# Agent / AI System

Creatures, NPCs, enemies and turrets are a prefab plus a stack of `IBehaviourModule` components; one [AgentController.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/Controller/AgentController.cs) ticks them and arbitrates by priority — never a behaviour tree, never an `AgentController` subclass.

**Scope:** `Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/` (namespace `SpaceGame.Agents`, Assembly-CSharp, no asmdef). **Related:** [EntitySystem.md](EntitySystem.md) (older overview — profile/module tables there are stale, prefer this file), [MountSystem.md](MountSystem.md), [Persistence.md](Persistence.md), [WeaponSystem.md](WeaponSystem.md), [NavMeshSystem.md](NavMeshSystem.md), [SkyTribe.md](SkyTribe.md) (the Sky Tribe's faction/roster/city/flying-war-party model in full — this file only points at it), skill [.claude/skills/spacegame-agent/SKILL.md](.claude/skills/spacegame-agent/SKILL.md) + its `reference.md`.

## Model
- Three decisions, one owner each: **who to fight** = [AgentTargeting.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/AI/Targeting/AgentTargeting.cs); **where to go** = [AgentGoal.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/AI/Goals/AgentGoal.cs); **how to move** = [IMovementMotor.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/AI/Motors/IMovementMotor.cs). A module duplicating any of the three is the bug this design prevents.
- Modules return `MoveIntent?`. `null` = pass to the next module. `MoveIntent.Idle()` **claims the frame** and starves everything below — and a suppressing condition (`Frozen`, `Foamed`) starves the whole stack before it is reached: `AgentController.Awake` ensures a `StatusReceiver` on its own GameObject and `Update` idles the motor while `StatusReceiver.Suppressed` holds, side-effect modules included, so a frozen creature cannot bite either. The receiver is ensured here because a prefab without a `StatusReactionModule` had none at all — Clanker, ClankerOutrider, CrabWalker6, HumanoidRobot and RobotHorse simply ignored the cryo sprayer — and on its OWN object, never through `StatusReceiver.Ensure`, which resolves to the networked root and would give a mounted rider its mount's conditions. See [StatusEffects](Artifacts/StatusEffects.md).
- `ClaimsMovement == false` modules are side effects: ticked unconditionally, must return `null`.
- Facing is a **second channel**: `IFacingModule` overwrites the winning intent's face target after arbitration — **only when its `FacingPriority` is strictly above the movement winner's `Priority`** (or it is the winner itself; nothing won → any). `AgentController.FacingApplies` is the rule.
- `AgentTargeting` + `AgentGoal` are auto-added in `AgentController.Awake`. Modules are discovered via `GetComponentsInChildren<MonoBehaviour>(true)`; discovery runs once, so a module added at runtime is never ticked (`RefreshModules` was deleted 2026-10-02 with no callers); put modules on the prefab.
- Riding (`MountModule` / `SteerModule` in [Modules/Riding/](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/Modules/Riding/)) rides on this stack but is documented in [MountSystem.md](MountSystem.md). A mounted NPC is the *mount's* agent with the rider as cargo whose movement — and only movement — is switched off (`NpcPassenger`): `ClankerOutrider.prefab` is a robot horse on the Clankers' side carrying a Clanker, built by [`RobotHorseBuilder`](Assets/Game/Editor/Creatures/RobotHorseBuilder.cs) beside the wild, Fauna `RobotHorse.prefab` from one component list. **Neither horse can attack anything** — no `CloseCombatModule`, no kick, no trample, no weapon, on either side. A horse is transport: the outrider's `ChaseModule` carries its rider to `RobotHorseBuilder.ChaseStopDistance` (inside `ClankerStack.GunMinRange`…`GunMaxRange`) and stands, and the Clanker in the saddle does the shooting. **Gotcha:** never "fix" a passive mounted enemy by arming the animal — that makes "kill the rider" and "kill the horse" one move with two animations and hides which half is dangerous (`GDC-L1-SYS-0005`, `GDC-L1-SYS-0006`). The fix is that a seated rider keeps its side-effect modules; mechanism and the bug it replaced in [Vehicles.md](Vehicles.md). An animal is made rideable and packable by **fitting a saddle** ([Saddles.md](Saddles.md)); the agent-side piece is [`SaddleSocket`](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/Modules/Riding/SaddleSocket.cs), which holds that state and enables the `MountModule` `Appa.prefab` carries **disabled**. **A Clanker is a stack, not a body.** [`ClankerStack.Apply`](Assets/Game/Editor/Agents/ClankerStack.cs) adds everything a Clanker does — capsule/agent from the body's bounds, motor, driver, brain, health 160, `ClankerFaction`, perception, the `Clanker.asset` targeting profile, patrol/formation/chase/search/keep-distance, two `NpcRandomLoadout`s + `NpcItemUseModule`, loot, alerts, provocation, hearing, streaming, netcode and savers — to whatever body it is handed as a `ClankerBodyFit` (animator, bounds, footprint radius, hand bone, run speed, three `AgentAnimatorDriver` numbers). Four bodies wear it, all built by `Tools/SpaceGame/Agents/Build Clanker Prefab`: the RPR `Clanker.prefab` ([ClankerBuilder](Assets/Game/Editor/Agents/ClankerBuilder.cs), Generic rig, own controller) and `PatrolRobot 1/2/3.prefab` ([ClankerBodyBuilder](Assets/Game/Editor/Agents/ClankerBodyBuilder.cs): Robert / Paperman / Engie, Humanoid on `Humanoid.controller`, 3.20 / 3.59 / 3.05 m). `ClankerBuilder.AllPrefabPaths` lists them; the town garrison, its `SettlementPopulation` and the spawn posse mix all four, the outrider's rider stays the RPR body. `PatrolRobot.prefab` and `DeathmatchBot.prefab` are not Clankers. Change Clanker behaviour in the stack, once; `ClankerPrefabTests` fails if a body's root component set drifts from the RPR Clanker's.

| `ProvocationModule` + `AggressionMath` + `MenaceSensor` 22 + `JostleSensor` 10 + `AggressionTelegraphModule` 12 | [ProvocationModule.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/AI/Targeting/ProvocationModule.cs) · [AggressionMath.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/AI/Targeting/AggressionMath.cs) · [MenaceSensor.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/Modules/Combat/MenaceSensor.cs) · [JostleSensor.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/Perception/JostleSensor.cs) · [AggressionTelegraphModule.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/Modules/Combat/AggressionTelegraphModule.cs) | Order −40. **A meter, not a flag.** `AddAggression(input, magnitude, from)` moves `Aggression` in [0,100]; `AggressionMath.BandFor` gives `Calm 0 · Wary 40 · Drawn 80 · Grudge attackAt`, scaled against this agent's own `attackAt` so a short-tempered creature still telegraphs. Reaching the top calls the unchanged `Provoke` — grudge, leash, `calmDownDelay`, alert — and below it `calmRate` drains it. **Or a jostle ladder** (`jostlesToFight` > 0, `FormerlySerializedAs("hitsToFight")`; 0 = the meter, every existing prefab, and `Jostled` is ignored): any blow ≥ `damageThreshold`, landed or blocked, is `Raise(Grudge)` — an instant, announced fight (GDC-L1-SYS-0006: one cause, one visible effect) — and only shoves climb. `JostleSensor` (side-effect module, so server-only) sweeps `SessionPlayers.Collect` (the spawned roster, or the local player offline, where `PlayerIdentity.All` is empty) every `scanInterval` 0.1 s and calls `Jostled(player)` for one whose horizontal distance ≤ own `CapsuleCollider` (else `NavMeshAgent`) radius + `contactSlack` 0.2 + the player's `PlayerMovement.BodyCapsule` radius, within `maxHeightGap`, closing faster than `minPushSpeed` 1 m/s, at most once per `jostleCooldown` 1.5 s — speed from position deltas, never collisions or velocities, which on the server describe only the host. Each jostle is one rung (`AggressionMath.LadderStep` — 3: Calm→Wary→Drawn→Grudge · 2: Calm→Drawn→Grudge · 1: Grudge); the top one is announced too. **`Announces(cause)`** is the one rule: every fight announces except one an `AllyHurt` input started. `settleSeconds` > 0 replaces the `calmRate` slope with steps — each band holds that long after the last provocation, then drops to the floor of the band below (`AggressionMath.Settle`/`FloorOf`); a ladder is forced to settle ≥ `LadderMinSettleSeconds` (5). Band-level entry points for code that reasons in bands: `Raise(atLeast, from, cause)` (never lowers; `Raise(Grudge)` fights even from a meter already waiting at the top), `Escalate(from, cause)` (one rung); `LastCause`/`LastCauseFrom` say why; `Settings` is writable (validated like the inspector). Points live in one serialized `AggressionSettings` block (`hitGain` 300 × damage fraction, `allyHurtGain` 40, `gunshotGain` 15, `menaceGainPerSecond` 20, `trespassGainPerSecond` 10 — Trespass is unfed until `TerritoryZone`); `damageThreshold` is a floor under `hitGain`, not a replacement. Fed by `HandleDamage`, `AlertReceiverModule`, `NoiseReceiverModule` (Gunshot **and** a hurt ally), and `MenaceSensor`, which sweeps `EntityTargetRegistry` every `scanInterval` for a player holding a `menacing` item, **who has fired a shot this agent heard within `brandishWindow` (6 s)**, and whose BODY faces within `aimConeDegrees` of this one inside `menaceRange` with line of sight, and feeds `Menace` per second past `menaceDelay`. The sweep starts at a random phase in `OnEnable`, and asks `HeardGunshotFrom` (a root compare) before the `EquipmentController` hierarchy search, so only the last shooter pays for it. Pointing a gun without firing is deliberately nothing: there is no aim verb, and facing somebody armed is also how you walk up to talk to them. At the defaults menace nets +10/s (20 in, `calmRate` 10 out) and stops 6 s after the last heard shot, so one shot and a held aim only reach Wary; it takes firing again — each shot another `gunshotGain` 15 — to reach the fight. `AggressionTelegraphModule` is the half the player sees — Wary faces and barks `warningLines` via `ChatterModule.TrySayNow`, Drawn raises `IsAiming` (unless `aimWhileDrawn` is off), returns `StopAndFace`, barks `lastWarningLines` and makes `DialogInteraction` refuse, Grudge yields to the ordinary fight and barks `provokedLines` if it has any (empty by default). Every bark goes through the agent's `ChatterModule` — without one the telegraph is mute. **Three traps.** The meter is server state, so bands replicate as `AgentAction.Band` on `NetMsg.AgentActed` (the new `NetArg.B`) or a client sees a nomad open fire with no warning at all. `AddAggression` no-ops once provoked, so an input arriving mid-fight cannot re-run the crossing and re-announce the alert. And `Provoke` sets the meter FULL, so an agent provoked by a route that skipped it — an alert, a script, a restore — does not read `Calm` in the middle of a gunfight. **Leave the telegraph off Clankers and Outlaws**: Hostile by stance, they never climb the meter |

Fed by `ProvocationModule` (Hit on every landed or fully-blocked blow, reported inline; before and regardless of the personal `damageThreshold` — a tribe notices you shooting its people whether or not the individual shrugged it off) and `HealthReactionModule` (Kill, **below** the `IsRestoring` guard or every reload re-charges the player for the same murder). ## Flows
**Per frame — `AgentController.Update`:**
1. `RefreshAuthority()` — if this machine does not own the agent: `TickPresentation(dt)` (`IPresentationModule` only) and **return**. Then `IsParked` (`Offstage` or `Dormant`) → tick the animator idle and **return** (motor parked when the flag went up). **`AgentController.Offstage`** (server, not saved): present but not acting — no module ticks (like `Suppressed`), the motor `ForceStop` + `SuspendSelfDrive`, and `AgentTargeting`/`PerceptionModule` skip their own `Update`. Never writes `enabled`. **`Offstage` is not saved and not replicated** — whoever sets it (the residents' routine) re-derives it after a load; clients never see it, which is fine because they run no modules anyway. It does not touch `EntityFaction` or the target already held. `Dormant` is the second parking reason, written only by `SimulationRange` ([SimulationDistance](SimulationDistance.md)); the two never release each other.
2. Bail if `Motor == null`. Build `AgentContext`.
3. Tick every side-effect module (`ClaimsMovement == false`) in discovery order, unconditionally.
4. Tick movement modules, priority DESC (ties by component order); **first non-null wins**. None → `MoveIntent.Idle()`.
5. `ApplyFacingOverride()` — `IFacingModule[]` by `FacingPriority` DESC, skipping any not above the movement winner's `Priority` (unless it is the winner); first `true` wins, overwrites `FacePosition`. **Facing no longer overrides a higher-priority movement winner (since 2026-10-01).** Melee (23) now beats the ranged aim (22) and the telegraph's facing, and `InteractionFocusModule` (Scripted 100) is never turned away mid-talk. A facing module meant to win must sit above the movement modules it competes with. **A holding goal keeps the frame for ever.** `GoalTravelModule` returns `StopAndFace` every frame on arrival, so `WanderModule` never runs; `Clear()` the goal (or set a non-holding one) to let the agent mill about. A new goal position always resets the hold, even 1 m away. **Hit ladder: `Raise`/`Escalate` set the meter to the band's FLOOR.** On the `calmRate` slope (`settleSeconds` 0) a floor drains out of its band within a frame — which is why `hitsToFight` > 0 forces `settleSeconds` ≥ `AggressionMath.LadderMinSettleSeconds`. A ladder Grudge provokes with `announce: false`: allies are not alerted by `AlertBroadcaster`; whoever opts into the ladder spreads the news itself. A blocked blow is always a graze, however hard it was swung.
6. Speed-variation drift applied to `SpeedMultiplier` (`MoveToPosition` intents only; phase is saved).
7. `Motor.Tick(intent, dt)` → `animatorDriver.Tick(Motor.Velocity, Motor.IsImmobile, intent.IsRunning)`.

**Execution order:** −100 `NavMeshAgentMotor` · −50 `AgentTargeting` · −40 `ProvocationModule` · 0 `AgentController` · 50 `LeggedDriver` · 100 `LeggedLocomotion` (asmdef `SpaceGame.Locomotion`) · LateUpdate `AgentAnimatorDriver`, `EntityEquipmentController` aim.

**Attack:** authority module fires → damages/spawns locally → `AgentActionRelay.Broadcast(this, AgentAction.Melee, origin, dir)` (`AgentAction.Ranged` keeps its wire number but has had no sender since the built-in agent gun was deleted) → peers `TryReadRay` and draw **presentation only** (no damage; `NetDamage` would bill the target once per machine).

**Caravan:** `NpcWorldSim` ticks `NpcGroup` records (lerp + `NpcTaskPlanner`) → player within `spawnRadius` → `NpcSpawn.Create` per `NpcGroupMemberSpec` in formation → members run their own AI; the leader resumes the record's errand in the record's phase (`NpcTaskModule.ResumeTask`, called after the goal is set: travelling keeps the task and the goal, dwelling finishes `DwellRemaining`, otherwise choosing — never `Choosing` up front: `NpcTaskPlanner.PickTask` avoids the current index, so the old `SetTasks` re-rolled on the first tick and overwrote the spawn goal, turning every caravan round when a player came into range; `CurrentDestinationName` is looked up from the record's `LastSiteId` via `WorldSiteRegistry.TryGet`, empty while that site's marker is unloaded) → `despawnRadius` folds them back into the record (`NpcWorldSim.ReadTaskBack`: task index, `LastSiteId`, remaining dwell; a leader that is not travelling clears the record's goal; a leader without tasks only gives back its index). **Wiped out is for good, for every group** (2026-10-05, user decision): once no `Live` member and no `Fighters` entry is standing (`GroupMembership.CountStanding` — a corpse still lying there counts as fallen, a member with no `HealthComponent` such as a walking city's house never falls, so the Strider city cannot be wiped out), `TickSpawned` sets `WipedOut` and the group is never spawned again in that world; `Record.wipedOut` saves it. Before, only a war party was marked, and a wiped-out caravan or herd folded back into its record and re-spawned at full strength — fresh guns, more loot — the next tick a player was in range, on the spot where it had been killed. A group that merely loses members still comes back whole after a fold — except a war party, whose fallen stay fallen (`NpcGroup.Fallen`, below). **War party:** goodwill reaches `AtWar` → `WarPartyDirector` opens a `War` and, once its cooldown clears, raises a `runtimeOnly` group at an unseen camp or a fallback point beyond staging distance, drawing its tier from the roster's `warPartyTiers` → the party follows a fuzzy trail fix refreshed after `trailInterval` without sight of its quarry — folded it catches up unseen to `staging + trailFuzz`; spawned, `NpcWorldSim.SteerSpawned` re-points the live leader (never a jump) → a quarry who leaves releases the party unresolved at once, and one never bound this session (a restored party) after `absentQuarryGrace` → Caught (this party's own `GroupMembership.GroupIdOf` dealt the blow: credited, which typically lifts the band out of `AtWar` and ends the whole war the same way decay or a crewmate's amends would — `WarNotice.GaveUp` fires from that band change, not from the resolve itself) / Defeated (`WarPartyRules.IsDefeated`; the sim marks `WipedOut` only when `IsWipedOut` holds — no `Live` member and no standing `NpcGroup.Fighters` entry, so a dismounted rider still fighting keeps the party in the field: credited, next party one tier stronger, war stays open, `WarNotice.Weakening` — **except a party of the roster's last tier: its defeat ends the war** (user decision 2026-10-07, every tribe; `WarPartyRules.EndsWar(outcome, tier, maxTier)`, read before `NextTier` clamps). `FactionGoodwillLedger.CreditOutOfWar` credits at least `defeatedCredit` and as much more as carries the row `WarPartySettings.peaceMargin` (1) past AtWar's exit (`GoodwillMath.CreditToLeaveWar`: `atWar + hysteresis + margin`, so −69 with the defaults), and the band change closes the war through `OnBandChanged` → `EndWar` (`WarNotice.GaveUp`, tier reset). The war is never closed in the book directly: under a band still AtWar, `Reconcile` reopens it on the next step) / Abandoned (beyond `maxPursuitDistance`: no credit, no notice — only this party gives up; the war stays open and its cooldown restarts; never while `NpcWorldSim.IsInFlight` — `WarPartyRules.ShouldAbandon(..., inFlight)` — since a flying party sets out from its home site wherever that is; running the catch-up first was tried and still abandoned a Sky party raised 1.6 km from a moving quarry). **Flying war party (`sky-war-party`, wired by `RosterAuthoring.WireWorldSim`):** `ChooseOrigin` returns the `WorldSiteRegistry.TryFindByName(transport.homeSiteName)` site (any kind, airborne, observed or not), else the usual camp/fallback → folded and undelivered the record moves at `transport.travelSpeed` and catches up as any party → inside `spawnRadius`, `NpcWorldSim.Spawn` counts the planned members, spawns `transport.VesselFor(count)` (skiff if its `VesselSeats.Capacity` fits, else freighter: Sky tiers 0 and 1 skiff, tier 2 freighter) (riders counted with `NpcGroupComposition.Rides`, the one predicate for count and seating; riders beyond the vessel's seats go on foot with a warning) — boarding an empty hull of that prefab parked `Done` at its dock within `dockReuseRadius` if one is there, else `NpcSpawn.Create` at the record's position, `RiseToCruise` before the network spawn, prow toward `QuarryPoint` — spawns and stamps the members exactly as on foot, under the hull (Live and Fighters from the start), and `VesselPilot.Begin(dock, QuarryPoint, riders, despawnRadius)` seats them; `QuarryPoint` is the director's lead, else the goal, else the group's position, and the dock is the group's `NpcGroupTransport.DockPoint` slot around the home site (rings `dockSpacing` apart, lowest free slot, kept by a reused hull), so parked hulls never overlap → while aboard the group's position is the vessel's → `TickTransport` (polled every sim tick, before the `WipedOut` return) sets `Delivered` (`NpcGroupTransport.IsDelivered`) once nobody is seated, or the hull is wrecked or gone — a run that came home with riders aboard (no landing site) is NOT a delivery: in view it waits `transportRetryDelay` at its dock and is re-`Begin`-ed (`ShouldRelaunch`), out of view the group folds with it and flies in again; from then the group is an ordinary spawned party and the empty hull is despawned once every player is beyond `despawnRadius`. Fold while aboard: vessel despawned FIRST (its `OnNetworkDespawn` puts riders on the NavMesh), then members; the record keeps `Delivered` false and flies on from the vessel's position. A released or wiped-out party is not removed (`ReleaseGroup`, the `RemoveAll`) while its `Transport` is still out. Restore: undelivered spawns in a vessel again, delivered on foot. With no usable vessel prefab the sim logs an error and spawns the party on foot (`Delivered` true). Full walkthrough with numbers: [SkyTribe.md](SkyTribe.md) Flows.

## Multiplayer
- The **owner** machine runs the whole stack; every other machine runs only `IPresentationModule`s. Ownership, not server-ness — [AgentAuthority.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/Core/AgentAuthority.cs). Modules must contain **no** authority check; the controller already gated the tick.
- Body transform + animator replicate through `NetworkObject` / [NetAuthority.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/Core/Multiplayer/Authority/NetAuthority.cs), which disables listed simulation drivers on remotes — motors and `NavMeshAgent`s ([SimulationDrivers.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/Core/Multiplayer/Authority/SimulationDrivers.cs)), **not** `AgentController`, which stays on to tick presentation modules. **`AgentController` stays enabled on clients (since 2026-10-01).** Before, `SimulationDrivers` listed it, NetAuthority switched it off on every remote copy, and `IPresentationModule` (ambient `ChatterModule`) never ticked off the host. A watching controller's first `Update` parks the motor (`ParkMotor`): it `ForceStop`s only a motor that is still **enabled** — the rigidbody motors stop by writing velocity, and a remote copy's kinematic body answers that with a console warning. Anything new that reads `AgentController.enabled` as "this machine runs the brain" is wrong: read `SimulatesHere`.
- Attacks replicate as `NetMsg.AgentActed` (69) via `AgentActionRelay`; damage stays on the deciding machine.
- `NpcWorldSim`, `SettlementPopulation` and `SettlementAlarm`'s rally are **server-or-offline** (`!Network.IsNetworked || Network.Server`), not `Network.Simulates(this)`. `NpcSpawn.Create` makes the same check itself.
- An `InventoryItem` an NPC carries needs its `itemPrefab` registered as a network prefab; projectiles and equipped visuals must **not** be. See [spacegame-multiplayer](.claude/skills/spacegame-multiplayer/SKILL.md).
- War: `WarPartyDirector` is server-only (`Network.Decides`), like `NpcWorldSim` beside it. `GroupMembership` is stamped in `NpcSpawn.Create`'s `beforeSpawn`, before `NetworkObject.Spawn`, so a member's `NpcRandomLoadout` roll is already seeded on spawn. A war cry replicates as `AgentAction.WarCry` with a line index, not the text, so every machine reads its own copy of the tribe's `hostileLines`. `FactionGoodwillNetwork.Notify` posts host-local rather than through `Send`'s skip-the-host rule — a notice is an event with nothing to hold, so skipping the host would mean the host never hears its own war party is coming. `EntityFaction` is not replicated, so `RosterValidation` requires every non-Rider member's prefab to already serialize the tribe's faction.
- **Folded groups seen from afar** are published by `DistantGroups` ([DistantGroups.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/Agents/World/DistantGroups.cs)) on the NetworkGameManager prefab — NpcWorldSim's object has no NetworkObject. Server-written `NetworkList<DistantGroupState>` (`GroupHash, TemplateHash, RosterSeed, Position, Yaw, Spawned`) every `publishInterval` 0.5 s, only for `showFromAfar` templates, only changed entries.

## Persistence
`AgentController` implements `IPersistentEntity`, so **every agent is save-eligible with no opt-in**. Savers live outside this tree in [Core/Persistence/Adapters/](Assets/Game/Scripts/Core/Persistence/Adapters/AgentStateSaveable.cs): `AgentStateSaveable` (key `"agent"` — target/lastAttacker as `SaveRef`, last-known position, `timeSinceSeen`, `profileId`, patrol index/direction), plus `PatrolSaveable`, `SearchSaveable`, `AlertResponseSaveable`, `NoiseInvestigationSaveable`, `AgentGoalSaveable` (key `agentGoal`; `hold`, `hasFacePoint`, `facePoint` appended — an older save loads as a non-holding goal); and the generic `TransformSaveable` + `HealthSaveable`. Add them inside the prefab's builder, then run `Tools/Save System/Wire Saveable Prefabs`. Details: [Persistence.md](Persistence.md). A group member is disowned from the world save (`NpcSpawn.Create`) because its group record rebuilds it; the one way back is `NpcWorldSim.ReleaseToPlayer`, when a player takes it (`SaveableEntity.ReclaimForWorld`). Seats that crew themselves on spawn (`ICrewedSeats`: `NpcPassenger`, `MountedGunners`) carry their stand-down across the reload through `CrewSaveable` (key `crew`). `ProvocationSaveable` (key `provocation`, deferred) holds `aggressor` (`SaveRef`), `calmingFor` and the appended `aggression` meter. **A record with an aggressor and no `aggression` field restores as a grudge (`AggressionMath.Max`)**: before the meter existed the saver wrote only provoked creatures, so the missing field means "fighting", not 0. Until 2026-10-02 it read as 0 and every such grudge (37 of 59 records in local saves) was forgiven on load. Covered by `ProvocationTests.AGrudgeSavedBeforeTheMeterExisted_StillHoldsAfterLoading`.

## Gotchas
- **A failed leader spawn no longer promotes a later `isLeader` member.** Places are decided before anything spawns (`GroupColumnLayout`), so the leader slot stays empty and the first member spawned leads (the fallback after the loop). Every template has one leader, so nothing changes in practice.
- **A settlement's siren can disagree with its defenders on a client.** `SettlementAlarm` asks goodwill before the table, but the ledger's rows and the player-to-profile bindings exist only on the server, so a client's copy answers by table alone: a player the tribe calls Allied hears the siren on their own machine while the server — which decides the rally — sends nobody after them. The rally is what matters; mirroring the siren would need the band on the wire. Until 2026-10-02 the server's query skipped goodwill too, and an Allied player walking into the tribe's own town was rallied against.
- **There is no legacy brain, and a dozen modules named in older comments no longer exist.** Deleted 2026-09-22 for having zero live instances: `NpcBrain`/`EnemyBrain`/`IAgentBrain`/`WanderBehaviour` (the four scene NPCs on them were re-authored onto `WanderModule`+`IdleLookAroundModule`+`InteractionFocusModule`, which is all they observably did — carrying no `EntityFaction`, their player reaction could never resolve a target), `CoverModule`/`CoverPoint`, `AirWanderModule`, `FlockingModule`, `ApproachModule`, `HuntModule`, `FacePlayerModule`, `TurretModule` (the live turret is `RocketLauncherTurret`), `WeaponMount`/`WeaponSelector`, `DuneRiderController`, `StrideStopProbe`, and the four `EntityProfile_*` authoring components with their editor. Their savers went too; `PursuitSaveable` now covers `KeepDistanceModule` alone, and `PatrolSelectionMode` moved to [PatrolModule.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/Modules/Patrol/PatrolModule.cs). **Deleted 2026-10-02 (agent restructure, phase 0)**, again for zero live users: the physics motors `RigidbodyMotor` (`FlyingRigidbodyMotor` stays: it flies the drifting Sky fleet) and `SteerModule`'s runtime motor fallback, `HorseDriver`, `IMovementMotor.NudgeDestination`/`SuggestDestination`, `MoveIntent.FacingDirection`, `AgentRangedCombatModule` with `agents/Weapons/` and `AgentProjectile`, `HerdModule` (+ its savers), `BasePatrolModule`, `HealthReactionSaveable`, `EntityAudioModule`, `EntityEquipmentController.autoUse`, `PerceptionModule`'s memory. Old saves still holding their keys load (unknown keys are ignored). An agent with no movement module idles and logs a warning. Build creatures from a builder in [Assets/Game/Editor/Creatures/](Assets/Game/Editor/Creatures). **Appa came back as NpcWorldSim groups, and three things had kept him out of the world (2026-10-04).** (1) Main's cleanup removed the scene instance and retired `AppaBuilder`; `Appa.prefab` is now the source and [AppaHerdAuthoring](Assets/Game/Editor/Creatures/AppaHerdAuthoring.cs) (`Tools/Creatures/Author Appa Herds`) only adds to it — `FormationModule` (empty id: inert until the sim names the band), `NpcTaskModule`, `GoalTravelModule`, a 10 m graze wander — then writes the `NomadAppa` (Fauna, saddled, a Sand nomad aboard, `HerdingModule`) and `NomadHerdAppa` (serializes **Sand**: `GroupMembership.Stamp` enlists a riderless member into its group's tribe on the server and faction is not replicated) variants. (2) The old `wild-appa` template started `startNearSite: AnimalGround` — and no `AnimalGround` site exists, so `ResolveStart` fell back to the sim's transform, (0,0,0), in the NavMesh-less padding at world x 0–1000; every roam point sampled there fails, so the record never moved and no player ever came within `spawnRadius`. Every herd template now uses `useStartPosition` on the NavMesh, and the id was retired (`appa-herd-dunes`/`-plateau`/`-south`, `sand-appa-herders`) because a save holding the old record would otherwise restore it at the origin — the sim skips records whose template is gone. (3) The old `HerdModule` (deleted 2026-10-02) could not key a herd per group (its `herdId` was baked into the prefab, so every Appa would be one herd); herds travel as a mob-shaped `FormationModule` band instead. **Add a band member's savers to the base prefab yourself**: `SaveableWiring.TryWirePrefabs` reaches `Agents/Caravan/` before `agents/creatures/`, so it gave each variant its own `NpcTaskSaveable`/`FormationSaveable` and then the base one more — two savers writing one key (`AppaHerdTests` fails on a duplicate).
- **Until 2026-09-07 nothing ever called `AlertBroadcaster.Broadcast`, so "alert allies" had never happened**, and the receivers only sat on the five robot prefabs. It is wired through `AgentTargeting.TargetAcquired` and `ProvocationModule.Provoke` now, but only for a target the agent found itself or was hit by: a target that *arrived by alert* is forced with `seen == false` and is never passed on, which is the whole cascade cap. Put both components on every member of a faction that should share a fight (the nomad builders do not yet), and never call `Provoke` with `announce: true` from anything that received the target second-hand. `callForHelpEvery` is the one deliberate exception (2026-10-02, the user's call for Raxys): it re-announces every interval, but only while the caller's enemy is inside its leash, so a fight spreads through allies standing near it — at most `alertRadius` past a fighter itself within its leash of the enemy — and dies with the fighters' calm-down; it cannot walk camp to camp. Test: `AlertChainTests.AFighterCallingForHelp_…`. Until 2026-10-01 the meter broke that itself: an `AllyHurt` gain that filled a meter provoked *announced*, so a camp whose `allyHurtGain` made one alert a fight relayed it to the next camp; `ProvocationModule.Announces` now keeps an alert-born fight quiet. `NoiseReceiverModule`'s aggro branch skips allies for the same reason: a `Hurt` noise carries the attacker, and a Clanker hearing a nomad scream must not turn on the Clanker that shot it.
- **Agents stand on the NavMesh, and the NavMesh is not the ground.** The world bake floats a median of 0.257 m above the terrain (max 0.600) — see [NavMeshSystem.md](NavMeshSystem.md). `AgentGroundConform` corrects it per frame. A constant `baseOffset` cannot: the error is terrain-dependent, so a single number leaves the body buried at one end of the range and floating at the other. Every agent prefab's `baseOffset` is 0 and should stay that way.
- **While a motor rides a link it owns the transform.** `NavMeshAgentMotor.IsRidingLink` (a ladder or plain link) and `IsLeaping` (a jump link) mean `updatePosition` is off and the body is driven by hand: anything that reads the agent's position as the body's, or conforms it to the ground, must stand down (`AgentGroundConform` does). Moving intents are ignored until the ride lands.
- **Never re-request a path while one is pending** (fixed 2026-10-04): `NavMeshAgentMotor.ApplyMoveIntent` re-called `SetDestination` every tick while `!agent.hasPath`, which is also true mid-computation, so a path needing more than one frame of `NavMesh.pathfindingIterationsPerFrame` restarted forever and the agent stood with `pathPending` true (a 1.1 km expedition leg; 60 m walked at once).
- **`baseOffset` has three authors now** — the prefab's own value, the ground correction and the mounted-jump arc — summed in `NavMeshAgentMotor.ApplyBaseOffset`. Assign `agent.baseOffset` directly from anywhere else and whichever writer runs later in the frame silently erases the rest.
- **Most agents are bipeds, so the slope lean defaults low** — `slopeFollow` 0.35 (`AgentGroundConformWiring.BipedSlopeFollow`); a biped spends the slope in its legs and leaning it like a many-legged body reads as falling over. The Golem is a biped (`Bone_Thigh/Shin/Foot_L/R`, nothing else), and so is the DuneRat despite quadruped bone names — 0.29 m forelimbs clear of the ground against a 0.99 m hind chain. Only the Vrescal hexapod gets `ManyLeggedSlopeFollow` (0.8). Applied **only when the component is added**, so tuning survives.
- **The node the slope lean is written to is animated on the Golem and the DuneRat and on nothing else.** `AgentGrounding.Baseline` works out which case it is by reading the transform back rather than from a per-prefab flag. Replace it with a flag and you get one of two silent failures: the lean erases the root-bone animation, or it multiplies into itself every frame and the body spins.
- **`AgentAnimatorDriver.animator` is empty on every nomad prefab by design — the driver finds it, so it must be asked, not assumed.** The Animator sits on the model child and the driver looks it up in `Awake`, but components on one object awake in no guaranteed order, so a caller's `OnEnable` can reach the driver first. Reading the field directly there hits the prefab's empty reference and throws `UnassignedReferenceException` on every nomad spawn; every entry point goes through `ResolveAnimator()`, the same lookup `Awake` uses. Do not write `animator?.Foo()` on any `UnityEngine.Object`: `?.` skips Unity's overloaded null check, so an unassigned field throws instead of being skipped.
- **No `EntityFaction` → invisible to every targeting module, silently.** Needs both a `FactionDefinition` and `GlobalRelationships.asset`. `EntityFaction.Ensure(go, faction, table)` on spawn paths.
- **Peaceful = no Hostile row, a `Neutral` `defaultStance`, AND a `Neutral` row toward every faction whose own default is `Hostile`** (today only `ClankerFaction`) + `ProvocationModule` (`leashRange` ≤ `AgentTargeting.loseRange`). Hostile is unilateral, so without that last row the Clankers' default makes the pair Hostile from BOTH sides and the "peaceful" creature attacks every robot it sees — the drifters did exactly that. Adding a Hostile row "for completeness" makes the whole faction attack on sight; so does a `Hostile` default, which is checked on every pair with no row. `FaunaFaction` has a Neutral default and its only rows are the two Clanker `Neutral` ones; `WildlifeFaction` is already Hostile to the player. **`FactionDefinition.wandering`** (set on `StriderFaction` and `SkyTribeFaction`) says the faction's towns move; anything that sends the player to a fixed place skips them (the lander's signal, [ShipSignal.md](ShipSignal.md)). **Only `ClankerFaction` sets `defaultStance = Hostile`** — that one checkbox is what makes them hostile to every people-faction, including ones added later that nobody remembers to write a row for, and their two `Neutral` rows toward Fauna and Wildlife are what keeps them off the animals (a row beats the default).
- **Three ways a "neutral until provoked" NPC half-works with a clean console**, all three found on the drifters at once. (1) No attack module: `ProvocationModule` + `ChaseModule` alone give a provoked NPC that walks up to you and stands there for ever — nothing ever swings. (2) No `ChatterModule`: `AggressionTelegraphModule` barks only through it, so the NPC turns to face you, plants its feet and says nothing. (3) An unarmed body with a `drawnAction` (the rifle aim) levels an empty pair of hands. `SculptCharacterBuilder` adds `CloseCombatModule` and `ChatterModule` and leaves the drifters' `drawnAction` empty; `CharacterActionWiring` gives them the `Point` wary gesture instead.
- **A creature turning on the spot reports no velocity, so nothing tells the animator it is turning.** A NavMeshAgent picking a new heading rotates the transform without translating it: `SpeedX`/`SpeedY` stay at 0, the blend tree sits on Idle, and the animal swings round with its feet planted like a turret. [`AgentAnimatorDriver`](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/Animation/AgentAnimatorDriver.cs) now also measures **yaw** off the transform — same reasoning as the velocity, so it works on a watching machine where only the NetworkTransform is moving the body — and publishes it as `TurnSpeed`, a signed rate in **degrees per second, positive turning right**. It is *optional*: the driver looks the parameter up once per controller and skips it where there is none, because writing a parameter a controller does not have logs a warning every frame per agent. Appa and the robot horse have turn clips so far. To add them to another creature: two looping in-place clips, a `TurnSpeed` float, and a blend tree with the left clip on the **negative** side. **A turn clip and `NavMeshAgent.angularSpeed` are one number, authored in two files.** The clip steps the feet through a fixed arc per cycle; the agent turns the body at whatever rate it likes, and the difference is skating. Appa's clips cover ~33° per 0.75 s cycle, so `AppaBuilder.TurnSpeed` is 45 °/s and sets both the agent's `angularSpeed` and the blend-tree thresholds. It was 130 before there was a clip to disagree with — three times faster than the feet are placed. The same derivation gives a *fast* mount its speed without skating: `RobotHorseBuilder` reads the stride the anim script prints (1.13 / 5.53 m/s at scale 1), multiplies by the scale and the `animatorSpeedScale`, and sets the agent speed and the blend thresholds to that product — 13.9 m/s at the gallop.
- **A collider parented to a BONE is 100x too big.** Every transform in an imported FBX carries `lossyScale = 100` (the centimetre convention — see [ArtPipeline.md](ArtPipeline.md)), and a bone is no exception. `AppaBuilder.AttachPetTarget` asked for a 0.75 m sphere 0.35 m along the muzzle and got a **75 m sphere centred 38 m away**; the player stood inside it, and a raycast that begins inside a collider does not report hitting it, so the pet prompt could never appear. Divide by `bone.lossyScale` (the builder sets `localScale = Vector3.one / boneScale` and then asserts the world radius came out right), and put the volume ON the bone rather than offset along it — a bone's local axes are whatever the export left them as.
- **`AgentTargeting.ForceTarget` has no self-check.** Anything that hands a creature its own transform gives it a target it can never lose and can never miss: it chases itself at distance 0 and melees itself forever, and to a player that is an animal losing health with no attacker anywhere. `NoiseReceiverModule` did not guard this, and now does. Guard at every call site, or in `ForceTarget` if a third one appears. **`HealthReactionModule.logDamage`** prints amount, source path and remaining health for anything that seems to be taking damage from nowhere. `HealthComponent.LastDamageSource` was always recorded and nothing read it back out.
- **A humanoid body's gestures, strikes and flinches are `CharacterAction`s, played through [`CharacterActions`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Presentation/Animation/CharacterActions.cs) — never an animator trigger.** The generated humanoid controller has no gesture triggers at all; each action has its own layer whose weight the component derives from the state, so a free-handed pet or wave no longer needs anything held up. Call `Play` wherever the event reaches; the component decides which machine writes the body ([HumanoidAnimation.md](HumanoidAnimation.md)).
- **A trigger collider only offers its interaction when the `IInteractable` is on that same GameObject.** [`Interactor.ResolveAlongRay`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Gameplay/Interaction/Core/Interactor.cs) treats a trigger as a detection volume rather than a surface: it never inherits an interactable from a parent, and the ray passes straight through otherwise. That is not a limitation to work around — it is the mechanism for a body-part interaction. [`PettableModule`](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/Modules/Social/PettableModule.cs) sits on a trigger parented to Appa's `head` bone, so "look at his head and press E" costs no raycast filtering and no distance test. Put the same component on the agent root instead and the body's solid collider answers for it, which offers "pet me" from every square metre of a 5.75 m animal including his tail and the underside of his feet. Parent it to the **bone**, not to the root, or the volume stays behind when he puts his head down to graze.
- **A sight line aimed at a target's ORIGIN is aimed at the ground, and the ground wins.** Every character's origin is at its feet, so `PerceptionModule` used to raycast from the eye to a point ON the terrain: the ray grazed every rise between the two and ended inside the ground itself, and the Clankers — 80 m of authored sight — acquired the player only inside `proximityAcquireRange` (4 m). `AimPointOf` now aims at the target's root-collider centre (or `targetAimHeight` above the origin), for sight and for muzzle checks alike; `PerceptionAimTests` stands a player behind a knee-high rise. **The project queries triggers by default (`queriesHitTriggers: 1`), so every NPC visibility ray must pass `QueryTriggerInteraction.Ignore`** — a ship's `BreathableAir` trigger hid a player 370 m away from 12 of 15 NPCs, and until 2026-09-17 nomads ran on unchosen 110°/35 m/5 s defaults; `PerceptionLineOfSightTests` covers both triggers and low cover. **`Network.Simulates` is the wrong gate for a scene rule.** It answers true on a client for anything without a spawned `NetworkObject` — a settlement root included — so `SettlementPopulation` called `World.Spawn` on a client 29 times in one session and was refused every time. Scene-level deciders use `Network.Decides` (server or offline); `Simulates` is for entities.
- **A creature's hearing range and its flee trigger are two different numbers, and the gap between them is a creature that ignores you.** A gunshot carries `Weapon.gunshotNoiseRadius` = **40 m**; `FleeModule.triggerRadius` on Appa is **22 m**. Shot from anywhere in between, he heard it, acquired the shooter through `NoiseReceiverModule`, went to `Mood.Fleeing` — and then kept walking his errand, because `FleeModule`'s own proximity hysteresis never flipped. Measured: shot from 30 m, and several seconds later he was **7 m closer to the gun**, at 0.62 m/s. He read as completely unbothered by being shot at. `triggerRadius` answers "did something frightening get *close*", which is the right question for a creature noticing a predator by itself and the wrong one for an alarm that arrived from further away. So [`FleeModule.Alarm()`](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/Modules/Movement/FleeModule.cs) sets the flag directly and `FightOrFlightModule` calls it on entering `Fleeing`: whoever decides the creature is frightened says so, and `FleeModule` decides where to run. Stopping is still `safeRadius`'s job, so an alarm cannot pin it running forever.
- **A dwell that lasts tens of seconds needs a bool, not a trigger.** `NpcTask.dwellFlag` names an animator bool that [`NpcTaskModule`](Assets/Game/Scripts/agents/Tasks/NpcTaskModule.cs) holds true for as long as the NPC works a site and clears the moment it moves on — `IsGrazing` on Appa's feeding task, which dwells 120–300 s. A trigger would fire once and leave the animal standing at attention for the rest of its meal. `SetDwellFlag` clears the previous flag before setting the new one; without that an animal that goes from grazing to drinking does both at once. Humanoids additionally gesture through `BodyLanguage` moments — speech, war cry, a task's end — fight with a cue moveset (`CloseCombatModule.attackCue`) and may block/dodge player melee (`MeleeDefense`) — see [HumanoidAnimation.md](HumanoidAnimation.md).
- **Out-ranking a module is not the same as switching it off, and for flee-vs-chase the difference is a creature that walks at the thing it is fleeing.** `FleeModule` (Override, 30) beats `ChaseModule` (20) *only on the frames it returns an intent*. It returns `null` whenever it cannot find a NavMesh point to run to — and `null` means **pass**, so the frame falls straight through to chase. Measured on Appa: nominally FLEEING, he covered 11.5 m toward the shooter, closed to 3.8 m and then legitimately tripped his own "cornered" rule. Two halves to the fix, both landed: `FightOrFlightModule` now enables flee and the combat modules as **mutually exclusive** sets rather than stacking them by priority, and `FleeModule.TryGetFleeDestination` fans out (seven directions × four distances) instead of sampling one point at full `safeRadius` and giving up. Any module pair where the loser would move the agent the *opposite* way needs the same treatment. **A lower-priority module never sees the frame a higher one lets go.** Movement arbitration returns on the first non-null intent, so a module is ticked only on frames nothing above it claims movement. `SearchModule` (19) started on a `hadTarget && !HasTarget` edge, but `ChaseModule` (20) claims every frame a target is held, so `hadTarget` was never true when Search ran: Clankers had never searched, and the save adapter that restored `hadTarget` was protecting a dead path. The edge now lives in `AgentTargeting.LostCount` and Search latches it (`handledLoss`). Any new "react when X stops" module must read a counter or a flag owned by the thing that changes, never its own previous-frame copy. `SearchSaveable` no longer writes `hadTarget` (old saves carry it; it is ignored on read). **Unity null hid a destroyed target:** `Target != null` is false for a target despawned, logged out or streamed away while held, so neither the non-viable drop nor `ClearTarget` counted it and the agent never searched live, while a reload of the same state did (`RestoreMemory`). Both use `ReferenceEquals` now (`SearchModuleTests.ATargetDestroyedWhileHeldIsSearchedFor`). A target destroyed on the very frame it dies is never seen dead, so it is searched for briefly too.
- **`FleeModule` on a peaceful creature finds nothing to run from.** Its default path resolves a threat by *faction relationship*, and a Fauna creature has no rows in `GlobalRelationships.asset` — so it is Neutral toward everything, the scan returns null, and the animal stands there being shot with a completely clean console. Set `fleeFromCurrentTarget`, which reads `AgentTargeting.Target` instead; `ProvocationModule` (damage) and `NoiseReceiverModule` (gunfire) are what put a target there. Do **not** "fix" it by adding a Hostile row toward the player — that makes every creature of that faction attack on sight.
- **Gunfire reaches AI through `Noise`, not through the damage path.** A shot that misses damages nothing, so `HealthComponent` never fires and nothing else would notice it. `Weapon.TryFire` calls `ReportGunshot` after a round actually leaves (not when a charge starts), an NPC's held gun goes through that same `Weapon` path (`NpcItemUseModule`), and the turret emits from `RocketLauncherTurret.Fire`. All of them are on the **deciding** machine only — `Weapon` by construction (`TryFire` is reached from `Use()` alone; `Present()` calls `Fire` directly), the others behind `authority.SimulatedHere`. Emitting on a peer would be heard by a copy of the creature that cannot act on it, while the copy that can heard nothing. `gunshotNoiseRadius = 0` makes a weapon silent to AI without silencing its sound.
- **A gunshot target does not stick on its own.** `NoiseReceiverModule`'s aggro branch is a bare `ForceTarget`, and `AgentTargeting`'s staleness pass drops it within seconds — for a Fauna creature nothing can ever re-acquire it, so the animal takes two steps and forgets. `FightOrFlightModule` re-feeds it through `ProvocationModule.Provoke` so the leash holds it. Anything else that force-targets a peaceful creature needs to do the same.
- **A script-added module keeps priority 0** — Unity does not call `Reset()` for `AddComponent`, so it ties with wander; set `priority` explicitly. And **two `AgentTargeting` components** — `[RequireComponent]` may already have added one; guard builders with a `GetComponent` null check. **A module returning `MoveIntent.Idle()` while merely waiting starves everything below it** — return `null`. **`*Builder` scripts in `Assets/Game/Editor/` overwrite prefabs wholesale** with no warning; hand-added components vanish on rebuild. `GolemBuilder` lost the Golem's `SaveableEntity` this way.
- **Feet skate** when only `animationSpeedMultiplier` / `walkAnimBoost` were tuned — those pick the *clip*. `animatorSpeedScale = groundSpeed / strideSpeed` sets the *rate*, applied once in `Awake`, and is global (attacks slow with it). **Provoked NPC walks instead of running:** `NavMeshAgent.speed` must be the **run**; scale `walkSpeedMultiplier` down from it, because `ChaseModule` asks for `isRunning`.
- **A humanoid NPC's melee is an action with a Contact mark, and the damage waits for it.** `CloseCombatModule.attackAction` (`Unarmed Strike` = UAL Jab/Cross; `Sword Strike` on the sword robot) starts on every machine with the variant in `AgentActed.B`; the authority lands the blow at `SecondsTo(Contact)` on its own clock, re-checking reach (`attackRange × rangeExitFactor`), and drops it if the tick comes more than `contactGrace` late (knocked down, dead, out-prioritised). `attackCommitDuration` is raised to at least the contact delay. It used to be `"Meele"` → a full-body spear throw at 2×, with damage 0.55 s before the arm got there. Creatures without an `attackAction` keep their trigger and immediate damage.
- **Creature controllers keep their own trigger names** (`Meele`, `Hurt`, `Death`, `Ram`…; `Golem.controller` carries both `Die` and `Death`). The humanoid controller has none of them — `CharacterActionWiring` empties those fields on humanoid prefabs and `HumanoidWiringAssetTests` fails any animator string a prefab's own controller lacks.
- **`PerceptionModule.occlusionLayers` left at `Nothing`** falls back with a warning and the agent shoots through walls intermittently. **A sight filter on `RaycastHit.transform` is a filter on the Rigidbody**: give a hull a root Rigidbody and every wall on it reads as the root, so `PerceptionModule` filters on `hit.collider.transform` (INVARIANTS: a query does not know what the solver was told). A **`LeggedLocomotion` left in `NetAuthority.simulationDrivers`** makes remote copies slide with still feet.
- **A module that throws is switched off, not the creature.** Every module tick (`AgentController.RunModule`) and every `TryGetFacing` (`ApplyFacingOverride`) runs behind the fault barrier — `Fault.TryEnter`, a local `try/catch`, `Fault.Report`; a throw is read as `null` — "I pass" — so the frame falls through to the next module instead of starving every module below the broken one. After 5 faults in 10 s that component's `enabled` goes false, so `IsActive` goes false and the controller stops ticking it for good; the creature keeps its other behaviours. `RunModule` returns `null` for a module that is not a `Component`, which every real module is. See [Diagnostics](Diagnostics.md).
- **The module loop allocates nothing per tick, and must stay that way.** It runs once per module per creature per frame, so one closure there was ~200 agents × ~10 modules of garbage every frame. That is why `RunModule` and `ApplyFacingOverride` use `Fault.TryEnter`/`Report` rather than `Fault.Run(() => …)` — do not "simplify" them back. `AgentModuleBarrierTests.RunningAModuleAllocatesNothing` measures 1000 calls at 0 bytes. To measure the whole agent stack, run the agent benchmark (`Tools/Agents/Run Agent Benchmark`, see [Diagnostics](Diagnostics.md)) before and after a change to the loop.
- **Provoked NPC walks instead of running:** `NavMeshAgent.speed` must be the **run**; scale `walkSpeedMultiplier` down from it, because `ChaseModule` asks for `isRunning`.
- **Every NPC death pays out, whoever did the killing, and what it leaves lies there for a while** (user decision 2026-10-05: "let the body lay there, and the loot, then let it despawn after a little while"). `EntityLootTable` drops the whole bag on any death — a Clanker its gun and the artifact it is carrying home, a Strider or nomad its gun — and empties it, so the corpse is not still holding the gun lying beside it; worn gear comes off too, except a **wing pack, which only a body killed while flying or a war-party flier sheds** (`DropsWorn`, user decision 2026-10-07), and a war flier (`MarkWarFlier`) drops its bag only on `NpcGroupTransport.flierWeaponDropChance`. Beyond `NpcWorldSim.DespawnRadius` of every player NPCs now sleep ([SimulationDistance](SimulationDistance.md)); fights still happen inside it and among group members, so the leftovers of fights nobody saw are still possible. The body stays (`HealthReactionModule.corpseLifetime`, 180 s) and each drop stays (`EntityLootTable.lootLifetime`, 180 s); then `Remains` takes each away through `GameServices.World.Despawn` (network despawn; a tombstone for an authored body) the first moment no player is within `unseenDistance` (150 m) — `UnseenRemoval`, the rule `AbandonedVehicle` shares. Server-only: clients see the despawn. A picked-up item is the player's; a player's own drop never gets a `Remains`. `RemainsSaveable` (key `remains`, on everything with health and every pickup, because the `Remains` itself is added at runtime) saves the time left, so a load pauses the countdown; a restored death starts a full one only if the saver put none back, whichever restores first. Group members are disowned from the save, so their bodies go with a fold or a load, never twice. The dead leave `EntityTargetRegistry` (`EntityFaction` unregisters on `OnDeath`, re-registers on `OnRevive`): a body lying for minutes would otherwise fill its town's population cap and keep its alarm raised.
