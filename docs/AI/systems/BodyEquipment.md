# Body Equipment

Gear you wear rather than hold: two gauntlets on the forearms, one item on the back, arranged
together with the three-slot hand hotbar on the gear screen. Since 2026-09-03 one of them carries
the game's **only light source** — see [Flashlight.md](Flashlight.md) — so an empty forearm is not
merely a missing ability, it is darkness. Design: [2026-09-02-body-equipment-design.md](../../superpowers/specs/2026-09-02-body-equipment-design.md), [2026-09-03-torso-slot-and-back-mount-design.md](../../superpowers/specs/2026-09-03-torso-slot-and-back-mount-design.md).

**Scope:** [Items/Body](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Body) (+ [Focus/](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Body/Focus)), [UseChannel.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Inventory/Core/UseChannel.cs), [DoubleTap.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Inventory/Core/DoubleTap.cs), [WornFit.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Equipped/WornFit.cs), [ForearmSeat.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Equipped/ForearmSeat.cs) / [WornSeat.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Equipped/WornSeat.cs), [BodyEquipmentSaveable.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/Core/Persistence/Adapters/BodyEquipmentSaveable.cs), [GearSaveCodec.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/Core/Persistence/Adapters/GearSaveCodec.cs), [BodyInventoryUI.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/Presentation/UI/Pages/BodyInventoryUI.cs), [GearRailLayout.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/Presentation/UI/Pages/GearRailLayout.cs), [GearTile.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/Presentation/UI/HUD/GearTile.cs), [Ghosts/](Assets/Game/Prefabs/Items/Equipment/Ghosts), [GearGhostBuilder.cs](Assets/Game/Editor/Items/GearGhostBuilder.cs), [ForearmBracers.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Body/ForearmBracers.cs), [ForearmBracerBuilder.cs](Assets/Game/Editor/Items/ForearmBracerBuilder.cs).

## Model

- **The bracer is permanent, and is not a slot.** Every player wears `gauntlet_base.blend`'s Mount variation on **both** forearms from the moment their body exists, seated by [`ForearmBracers`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Body/ForearmBracers.cs) through the same `ForearmSeat.Apply` a gauntlet goes through — so **a gauntlet is only the device standing on its hardpoint deck.** Until 2026-09-04 each gauntlet model carried its own copy: putting one on swapped a whole forearm, taking it off left a bare sleeve, and seven copies of one armour shipped. It holds no state, is on no wire and is in no save.
- **Three worn slots** — [`BodySlot`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Body/BodySlot.cs): `Torso = 0`, `LeftGauntlet = 1`, `RightGauntlet = 2`. Values are persisted and on the wire; append only. The *names* are free — both carry the index — which is how `Back` became `Torso` without touching a save.
- **Every item has an [`EquipKind`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Core/EquipKind.cs)** on its `InventoryItem` asset: `Hand` (default), `Gauntlet`, `Back`, `Chest`. It is on the *asset*, not the prefab, because the server decides what fits where without instantiating anything. Gauntlets today, seven: Grappling Hook, Sucker Puncher, Leash, Item Scanner, Ruin Scanner, Repulsor Gauntlet, Flashlight Gauntlet. Back: Wing Pack and **Wingsuit** — two ways to fly that are mutually exclusive for free, because there is one torso slot and both want it ([Wingsuit.md](Wingsuit.md)). **Chest: nothing yet** — the slot ships ahead of its content; see Extending.
- **What fits where is one pure table**, [`BodySlotRules`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Body/BodySlotRules.cs): the torso takes `Back` **or** `Chest`, either gauntlet slot takes `Gauntlet`, a hotbar slot *stores* anything. The hand only *equips* `Hand` — selecting a gauntlet in the hotbar empties the hands and the tile shows a small disc in its corner.
- **One torso slot, two places.** Back gear clips to the expedition rig's **lash rail** — the bar across the folded pack whose ends stick out past each flank — and chest gear sits on the chest bone. Which one an item takes is read off its `EquipKind`, never chosen by the slot, so *"you can have one or the other, never both"* needs no rule at all: there is a single slot, and putting either in displaces the other through the ordinary swap. [`WornSeat.BoneFor`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Equipped/WornSeat.cs) is the one place that choice is made, shared by the wear and the ghost.
- **Back gear's position is measured off the rail, not authored.** [`BackpackController.GearMount`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Backpack/BackpackController.cs) hands out the `Mesh_Rig_LashRail` transform of the *worn* pack, and `WornSeat.Apply` puts the item's origin on it — so the fit's `localPosition` is only the fallback for a back with no pack on it. The item is still parented to the **spine bone**, never to the rail: the pack leaves when it is deployed, and anything parented to it would go too.
- **A slot in either list is a [`GearRef`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Body/BodySlot.cs)** (`GearArea.Hotbar`/`Body` + index). [`UseSlotCode`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Body/UseSlotCode.cs) packs it into `NetArg.A` as `area << 8 | index`, so every hotbar code is still the bare slot index it always was.
- **The hotbar is three slots** (`inventorySize: 3` on the networked player). **The gear screen is a step-out into the world**, not a panel: a spawned [`BodyFocusCamera`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Body/Focus/BodyFocusCamera.cs) frames the whole figure from the front, level (see below for the shot's numbers), three [`BodySite`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Body/Focus/BodySite.cs)s sit on the body at the same transforms the worn items use, and the flat canvas holds nothing but a header, the carried icon and **all six slots as a pyramid down the LEFT edge** (user, 2026-09-04 — the hand tiles were a row along the bottom until then, which lay across the legs of a figure the lens frames whole; user, 2026-09-06 — the three worn slots joined them). The rows read top-down the way the body does: torso, the two gauntlets, then the hands, one row of 1 / 2 / 3. **The sites on the figure stay exactly as they were** — this is a second route to the same three worn slots, not a replacement, and the two are wired together: pointing at a worn tile lights its site on the character (`BodyFocusSession.SetExternalHover`), and clicking either sends the identical request. Only a refusal differs, and only in where it is shown — on whichever of the two was clicked. Geometry is [`GearRailLayout`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Presentation/UI/Pages/GearRailLayout.cs), which is arithmetic and nothing else so the shape can be asserted. (The per-site key chips and the caption live on `WorldOverlay`, not on the canvas.) **All three sites signal the same way**: a translucent ghost of what goes there, on the spot it goes — a faint blank device standing on the real bracer's deck on an empty forearm (it was a ghost *bracer* until the bracer became permanent, at which point it was a translucent copy of a solid thing already on the arm), and `GhostBack`'s mount frame on an empty torso, seated on the pack's lash rail by the very call that wears real gear, so it stands over the shoulders. While carrying, every site the item can legally go to shows a translucent copy of it seated where it will sit, or an amber outline on what is already there for a swap. Design: [2026-09-02-body-screen-in-world-design.md](../../superpowers/specs/2026-09-02-body-screen-in-world-design.md).
- **One heading, and the lens never turns.** It used to swing 180° round to the pack whenever a `Back` item was picked up, because the lash rail cannot be seen from the front. That premise died with the worn models: the torso's ghost is a frame over the shoulders and its preview is the item's own gear-screen model — a spread pair of wings — and both read from the front. The swing cost the player their bearings, hid the face, and took both forearm sites out of reach mid-carry, so it is gone along with `OrbitTo`, `FacingBack`, `Heading` and `Reachable`. `GDC-L1-UX-0004` is what argued for it and what argues against it now: the signifier should be the thing you are putting gear on, and that thing is visible where the player already is.
- **Firing.** Q/E press and release the left/right gauntlet; a double tap of Space (`DoubleTap`, 0.3 s, unscaled) presses-and-releases the back. Every one of the four triggers — hand included — is a [`UseChannel`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Inventory/Core/UseChannel.cs): the request → present → authority → broadcast pipeline plus the 15 Hz hold stream, extracted from `EquipmentController` so all four replicate through one code path.
- **The firing arm comes up.** A gauntlet use raises that arm, extended at what the player is looking at, through three raise STATES on the Upper Body layer (`Raise Left` / `Raise Right` / `Raise Both`, entered on the `ArmRaise` int; each a 1D blend tree over the `AimPitch` float of authored `Point` clips at -45/0/+45° look pitch, the left one the right clips mirrored) — on every machine, for a short linger after a tap or for as long as a held item streams. `PlayerAimRig.RaiseArm` writes the parameters; the clips come from `gauntlet_point.py` in the astronaut's source folder and the states from `Tools/SpaceGame/Player/Build Upper Body Layer`. The decision is driven by `UseChannel.Presented` / `HoldPresented` — the points where the use is *shown* — so a peer's copy of the press raises the same arm the wearer's does, with no message of its own. [`ArmRaiseLatch`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Characters/Player/Combat/ArmRaiseLatch.cs) is the pure timing.
- **Worn and carried can be different MODELS, and since 2026-09-05 there are THREE forms.** [`WornVisual`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Equipped/WornVisual.cs) swaps an item between the thing it looks like in a hand (`Carried`), on a back out in the world (`Worn`, child `WornModel`) and on the gear screen (`Inspected`, child `InspectModel`). Both back items use the first two: the wing pack is a folded aircraft in the hand and a pair of wings on the rig's bar tips when worn; the wingsuit is a bundle of cloth on a spar in the hand and a wing spread between the arms when worn. **Only the wing pack has the third**, and `Inspected` falls back to `WornModel` for everything else, which is why nothing else had to change: stowed it is 2.64 m across (`WornFit.size`) so a walking character is not wearing a wingspan, and spread on the gear screen it is 5.51 m (`inspectSize`) because that screen is the one place a player looks *at* their own back on purpose. Both come out of one generator at one scale on the same rail-tip origins, so the swap moves nothing; `BodyFocusSession` asks `BodyEquipmentController.SetTorsoForm` for `Inspected` on open and `Worn` on close. Clutter off the walking silhouette, wings kept where they read (`GDC-L1-UX-0003`, objective/4 — *reduce clutter; every element competes for attention*). The swap is **by child name and by no component of its own** — the gear screen previews worn items with `DisplayCopy` ghosts, and a display copy has every MonoBehaviour taken off it, so a component holding the references would be gone exactly when the ghost needed it and the ghost would promise the carried model. `WornSeat.Apply` performs the swap **before** it measures, so the size sizes the showing model.
- **The gear screen looks at the body in its own idle — arms down — and only the gear that needs it takes them out.** [`InspectStance.Apply`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Body/Focus/InspectStance.cs) is written from `BodyFocusSession.LateUpdate` **only while the torso holds, or the cursor carries, an item whose [`WornFit.HoldsArmsOut`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Equipped/WornFit.cs) is set** — today the **wingsuit alone**, whose membranes are lofted along `InspectStance.DefaultDroop` (45°) and fold into the ribs with the arms at the sides. Everything else — wing pack, mount frame, all seven gauntlets — stands clear of the arms and reads in the idle, which is how the character stands everywhere else in the game (user, 2026-09-04; the screen held every body out at 45° until then). **Holding the arms out is not a fix for gauntlet clicks landing on the torso** — that was tried on 2026-09-04 and did not work; the hit regions are what decide that, see the Gotchas. The flag is on the **item's prefab**, read off the prefab rather than off the worn instance or its ghost, because both have been stripped by then. `BodyFocusSession.armsOut` is the master switch over the whole mechanism; `armDroop` is the angle it uses when gear does ask. The stance itself is unchanged: two aimed bones per arm rather than a clip — "point this segment that way in the world", so it needs no authoring and works on any humanoid rig — and nothing is restored on the way out, because pose writes are not state: stop making them and the Animator owns the arms again next frame. **45° is still one number in three places** — `InspectStance.DefaultDroop`, `BodyFocusSession.armDroop` and `wingsuit_worn.py`'s `INSPECT_DROOP`: move one alone and the cloth floats off the arm.
- **The screen frames the WHOLE figure, from slightly above.** The shot used to be the chest from 2.3 m at a 40° FOV, which covered 1.67 m of a 3.25 m astronaut — head and legs both cut off, which stopped mattering the moment worn gear reached from the shoulders to the knees. It is 4.4 m at 44° now (3.55 m of coverage), with the lens half a metre above the chest and tipped 9° down so the figure sits centred. In a tight space the wall probe still pulls the lens in and the framing still loses the ends of the figure; that is pre-existing and unfixed.
- **Worn is the same artifact.** A worn instance runs the item's own `OnEquipped`, state restore, `Use`/`Present` split and hold stream unchanged; the only difference is `UsableItem.Worn = true`, set before `OnEquipped`, which skips the hold pose so the hand stays free.

## Flows

**Bracer** — per arm, in `LateUpdate`, as soon as that arm is ready: read `BodyEquipmentController`'s own resolved sites (`ForearmBone`, `HandSocket` — never a second resolution), instantiate `ForearmBracer.prefab` on it, seat through `ForearmSeat.Apply`. Both arms dressed and the component disables itself; five seconds without them and it says so, loudly, once. No teardown, no message, no save.

**Wear** — 1. `BodyEquipmentNetwork` list changes (server write, late-joiner adopt) → `OnBodySlotChanged` on every machine. 2. `BodyEquipmentController.OnSlotChanged` strips the old instance (write back state, end hold, `OnUnequipped`) and wears the new. The controller instantiates and sanitizes; **where the thing sits is [`ForearmSeat`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Equipped/ForearmSeat.cs) and [`WornSeat`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Equipped/WornSeat.cs)**, which the body screen's ghosts also go through. `ForearmSeat.Apply` straps a gauntlet to the **forearm bone**: origin at the wrist minus `wristGap`, the model's -Z along the wrist-to-elbow line, its +Y along the back of the arm (the hand frame's thumb side × the arm axis, mirrored for the left), `cuffScale` across the arm and `lengthScale` along it (both 1, divided by the bone's own lossy scale), and a negative X scale on the left arm so the latches stay on the little-finger side. The hand socket from `EquipmentController.NewSocket(hand)` is still built, but only to read the thumb side off its grip frame — nothing is seated in it. `WornSeat.Apply` puts the torso item on the bone `BoneFor` picked — spine for `Back`, chest for `Chest` — scaling from a **measured** `ItemBounds` so the fit's `Size` means "draw it this big". Given a mount (the worn pack's lash rail, for back gear only) the *position* comes off that instead of off the fit; the rotation and size are the fit's either way. 3. `usable.Worn = true` → `OnEquipped` → `RestoreItemState(slot.State)`. 4. If the change was `Deliberate`, `Celebrate` plays the equip sound at the instance and taps the arm's raise latch.

**Fire** — Q down → `OnGauntletPressed(Left)` → left channel `Press()` (mounted or not): `A = UseSlotCode.Encode(Body(LeftGauntlet))`, `OnRequestUse`, `PlayUse`, owner `TryUse` if owner-authoritative, `NetToServer(UseItem)`. Continuous items stream hold ticks from `Tick`; Q up → `Release()`. Server: `EquipmentController` and `BodyEquipmentController` both receive the message on the player's relay; each forwards only codes in its area (`UseChannel.Owns`). Body guard: the decoded slot still holds an item. Double Space → back channel `Press(); Release();`.

**Move** — I opens `BodyInventoryUI` (own `InputControls` UI map; `GameplayMenuScope.Enter(freezeTime: false, hideHud: true)`; refuses with no local player, while mounted, while another gameplay menu holds the scope, or while typing), which calls `BodyFocusSession.Enter()`: the camera flies from the eye to the front of the chest (0.4 s), `WorldOverlay.EyeOverride` is pointed at it so labels project through the lens that is rendering, and the three sites are built off `BodyEquipmentController`'s own bones and sockets. Click a filled tile or site → carried, locally (**a worn slot has both**, and the two are interchangeable); `SetCarry` re-resolves every site through `BodySiteState`. Click a legal target → the site is lit `Committing`, **then** `IBodyEquipment.RequestMove(from, to)` → `MoveServerRpc` → `GearMoves.Resolve` on the server → both lists written (hotbar half via `IPlayerInventory.TrySetSlot`) → slot events redraw tiles and sites. Click a red site or a red tile → shake + `UiError` **on the one that was clicked**; click the world → the carry goes back. Close (I, Esc, death, despawn) → `Exit()` disposes every ghost, un-hides the renderers it hid, hands `EyeOverride` back and flies the camera home (0.25 s). Nothing moves locally.

**Hotbar selection of a worn kind** — `EquipmentController.HandleEquip` checks `BodySlotRules.HandEquips`; a gauntlet or the wing pack in the selected slot leaves the hands empty. Drop (G) still works from the hotbar; that is how a worn item leaves the body: move it down to the hotbar, select, drop.

## Multiplayer

- Body slots are **server state** (`NetworkList<FixedString64Bytes>`), owner-permission RPCs, late joiners adopt in `OnNetworkSpawn` — the hotbar's shape exactly.
- Wearing is **derived from replicated state on every machine**, never sent; the worn instance is a plain `Instantiate` on a bone (its own `NetworkObject` dormant), so `Network.Simulates(worn)` is true everywhere — ask about the owner, as for held items.
- Use messages are the hotbar's four (`UseItem`/`ItemUsed`/`UseItemHold`/`ItemUseHeld`); the area bits in `A` route them. No new network prefabs.
- **The bracer is derived, not replicated.** Every machine runs `ForearmBracers` for every player body it has, local or remote, and produces the same two children from the same prefab — so peers see both bracers for nothing on the wire. There is no state for two machines to disagree about; "everyone wears two" is a rule, not a value. Nothing is spawned, so nothing needs registering in the network prefab list.
- **The body screen sends nothing new.** The camera, the ghosts, the hit rects and the hidden renderers exist only on the machine of the player who opened it — a peer sees that player standing still — and a move leaves through `RequestMove`, which already has its RPC. The ghost prefabs carry no `NetworkObject` and are never spawned, so there is nothing to register. The wear feedback (equip sound + arm flex, `BodyEquipmentController.Celebrate`) is *derived* from the replicated slot change on every machine, so a peer sees the flex for nothing on the wire; it is suppressed during the initial adopt.
- **Verify on a real client:** wear a gauntlet and see it on the host; swing on Q; see the host's gauntlets; move through the gear screen; double-Space wings. For this change specifically: on a client, carry the wing pack and watch the lens swing to the pack and the rail light up, then confirm the host sees the wing pack land **on the rail**, not floating off it.

## Persistence

- `BodyEquipmentSaveable`, key `"body"`: `itemIds` positional by `BodySlot`, `itemStates` positional, omitted when empty — through `GearSaveCodec`, which `InventorySaveCodec` now also uses. Capture writes back every worn instance's state first (`WriteBackWornState`); restore is `RestoreSlots` → bags → `ReapplyWornState` → `OnLoadComplete` asks every worn `IItemDeferredRestore` (the wing pack's craft).
- **Old saves with four hotbar entries.** `InventorySaveCodec.Restore` hands entries past the bar's width to an overflow callback; the hotbar saver queues them on the body (`QueueOverflow`), and `BodyEquipmentSaveable.OnLoadComplete` drains the queue into free matching slots *after* every saver has restored, warning by name for anything that fits nowhere. Before this, `RestoreSlots` dropped them silently.
- Per-instance state is **not carried across a move** — see Gotchas.
- **The bracer is not saved, deliberately.** It holds no runtime state: it cannot be removed, swapped, damaged or moved, so a saved copy of it could only ever be right or corrupt. It is rebuilt from the prefab on every spawn on every machine. The gauntlets standing on its deck are saved by `BodyEquipmentSaveable` exactly as they always were.
- **The body screen holds no state of its own.** It is a view over the two saved lists: every ghost dies with the session, and what the player is wearing is saved by the body slots. Nothing here needs a saver.

## Gotchas

- **The double-Space means "use the torso item", not "deploy the wings".** Two items answer it now and they do different things: the wing pack spawns a craft and mounts you in it, the wingsuit flies your own body and toggles. Anything that reasons about the gesture has to go through the item, not around it.
- **Gauntlets fire while riding and flying; the back item does not.** Q and E are nominally the mount `Turn` axis, but no shipped mount binds it (`SteerModule.turnActionName` is empty on every prefab; the ornithopter yaws off `Move.x`), so a gauntlet press has nothing to fight and `BodyEquipmentController` lets it through mounted. The double-Space is dropped while `IBodyEquipment.IsMounted` (`GetComponentInParent<MountModule>()` — a rider is parented into the seat): airborne, that gesture is the ornithopter's flap, and a craft already under the pilot must not spawn another. A grapple fired from the saddle draws its rope but cannot swing the rider — the mounted body is kinematic, and its constraint is silently ignored. If a mount ever binds `Turn`, `OnGauntletPressed` is the seam that has to arbitrate.
- **A move resets the item's `ItemState`.** NetworkList writes arrive per index and assigning `InventorySlot.Item` clears the bag on every machine; the backpack has the same rule. The one state that would matter — a deployed wing pack — is protected by `GearMoves` refusing every move while mounted.
- **`Worn` must be set BEFORE `OnEquipped`.** It is the only thing that stops a gauntlet adding a `HoldAnimator` and posing the arm as if gripping. `WornPoseTests` pins it.
- **The controller only writes state back when the slot still holds the item it wore.** A move changes the slot's item before the strip runs; without the check the old item's bag would land on the new item.
- **Two components register the same four `NetMsg`s on one relay.** `NetChannel` keeps a handler list per id, so this is fine — but each must forward only its own area (`UseChannel.Owns`), or a hotbar press fires a gauntlet.
- **The screen is on I, not F.** It moved on 2026-09-03, when interact took right mouse and freed I up — see [InteractionSystem](InteractionSystem.md). `UI/BodyInventory` carries the binding; F is now bound to nothing.
- **The gear screen refuses to open while mounted, and while any other gameplay menu holds the controls** (`GameplayMenuScope.IsActive`; `PackFocusSession` guards the same way) — and it drops its carry on any slot change it did not make (the world moved something under it). Without the scope guard, opening it over a deployed pack takes the scope a second time — so it is not handed back until both owners exit — and spawns a second focus camera, and two enabled cameras at one depth render in no defined order.
- **`session.Commit` must be called BEFORE `RequestMove`, not after.** On a host the move RPC runs synchronously — NGO picks `LocalSendRpcTarget` when `IsServer` and handles the message inside the call, and the `NetworkList` write raises `OnListChanged` from there — so with the natural-looking order the answer arrives and clears the pending commit *before* the commit is set. The site then sits showing its preview ghost over the real item for the whole `commitTimeoutSeconds` (1 s) and ends with a refusal shake, at a player whose equip in fact succeeded. Commit-first is correct on both sides: on a host the answer clears it on its way past, on a client it stays lit across the round trip. The timeout is the deadline for the case that announces nothing — a *refused* server move sends no reply at all.
- **Only the OWNER of a body may open a session on it.** `BodyFocusSession` sits on every player prefab, including the replicas of the three players standing next to you, so `Enter` guards on `Network.Owns` before anything else. Without it a stray `Enter` flies a camera to somebody else's chest to rearrange gear the server would refuse to move. `Owns` answers true offline, which is right: single-player is a host of one.
- **Two traps in putting worn slots on the rail, both introduced by it.** (1) A `GearRef`'s `Index` is its index within its OWN list, so `Torso` is 0 and hotbar slot 1 is 0 too: `tile.Slot.Index == hotbar.SelectedSlotIndex` lit the torso tile amber and lifted it off the rail every time slot 1 was in hand. Both per-tile state reads are guarded on `IsHotbar` for this reason — the other is the "worn kind in the wrong place" badge, which on a body tile would badge an item that is exactly where it belongs. Compare `GearRef`s whole, never by `Index` alone. (2) A cursor over the UI stops the body's own hit-test dead — `UpdateHover` takes `overUi` and answers "no site", correctly, or the figure behind a tile would be hovered through it — so a worn tile has to *tell* the session which site it means, via `BodyFocusSession.SetExternalHover`. The session then raises `HoverChanged` as usual and the ghost, the outline and the caption come out of the ordinary path. `externalHover` is only consulted while the cursor is over UI, so it can never fight the figure for a hover; it is cleared on both teardown paths beside `hovered`.
- **The UI polls `session.IsOpen` every frame; the session deliberately raises no `Closed` event.** The world half ends on its own for death, despawn and `OnDisable`, and those are teardown paths where calling outward is what bites. So `BodyInventoryUI.Update` asks instead, and closes itself. Delete the poll and the screen sits open holding `GameplayMenuScope` over a world with no camera: a cursor, no controls, nothing to close.
- **What a site DRAWS and what it can be CLICKED at are two different things, and a click is ranked by the CARRY before any geometry.** [`BodySite.HitBoxes`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Body/Focus/BodySite.cs) is the seam. A gauntlet site and the torso's **chest** place are clicked where they are drawn — compact things sitting on the spot they belong to, already tight targets. **The torso's `Back` place is the exception and the reason the seam exists**: it draws something metres across (a mount frame over the shoulders, a wing pack's wings down both flanks, a wingsuit spread between the arms) whose box swallowed both forearms, so a click aimed squarely at a gauntlet went to the torso. It is clicked at **the worn pack's lash rail's two protruding ends and nowhere else** — two boxes of `BodyFocusSession.torsoHitMetres` (0.28 m) half-width on the ends of `Mesh_Rig_LashRail`, via `BodyEquipmentController.TorsoMount` (user, 2026-09-04: *"you only can interact with the torso item by clicking on the chest or on the two bars on the backpack. nothing else"*). Those ends are the only part of the rail a front-on lens can see — the span between them is buried in the rack — so **what is clickable is what is visible**. Rig deployed, there is no rail: one box on the spine keeps the slot reachable rather than handing the ghost its old rect back. The bar's long axis is **found** (its renderer's longest local extent), never assumed — which local axis runs along it is an artefact of the FBX's axis conversion. Hit-testing is still screen-space rects and not colliders (three sites do not justify them, and a trigger near the player is a thing the movement probes, the scanner and other players' rays all hit): every box projected and padded by `hitPaddingPx` (12), each kept as **its own rect**, never merged, so two rail ends with a whole back between them do not become one box across the shoulders. Disabled renderers are skipped, which is what makes a `Reserved` site measure the placeholder rather than the hidden item, and corners behind the near plane are skipped rather than failing the whole site. `TryCanvasRect` returns the union and is for the **chips and caption** — somewhere to hang, not somewhere to click; `TryCursorHit` is the click. Then [`NearestSite`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Body/Focus/NearestSite.cs) ranks: **what the cursor carries first** — carrying a gauntlet the two arms win and the nearer takes it, carrying a torso item the torso does (user, 2026-09-04), answered by `BodySlotRules.Accepts` so the hover and the move's own verdict cannot disagree; outranked is **not** unclickable, since a site the carry cannot go to must still take the click when it is the only one under the cursor or there is nothing to shake. Under that, **DEPTH: nearest the lens wins** — nearest-rect-centre alone shipped wrong (the ornithopter-wing case above), and `DistanceFrom` measures box **centres**, not nearest faces, because a box's near corner reaches past whatever is in front of it. Within `TieMetres` (0.1) aim decides after all: the arm-across-the-chest case, and what picks the nearer of two arms, which sit at equal depth on a front-on lens. **The rail boxes still OVERLAP the arms on screen and are meant to** — measured 2026-09-04, the ends sit 4.95/5.11 m from the lens against the forearms' 4.45/4.66 m, so a forearm drawn in front of a rail end wins that pixel on depth by 0.3–0.5 m, which is correct: what is in front is what you are pointing at. The clear band above and outboard of each arm is what grabs the back item with an empty cursor.
- **A ghost is seated by the PREFAB's fit component, never by anything on the copy.** `DisplayCopy.Strip` takes every MonoBehaviour off, so `BodySite` reads the `GauntletFit`/`WornFit` from the prefab and hands it to `ForearmSeat`/`WornSeat`. Ask the copy and it answers null, and the ghost sits at the bone at the wrong size — promising a place the gear does not land. A gauntlet prefab with no `GauntletFit` cannot be previewed at all, the same rule the controller already enforces when wearing one.
- **An outline material may not be shared between two shells.** `OutlineShell.Build` writes `_OutlineWidth` onto the material it is handed, computed from the traced visual's own size, so two visuals tracing from one material both render at whichever width was written last. Each `BodySite` therefore owns its own `Palette` of three rim materials and disposes them with the site.
- **Hidden renderers are restored on every exit path.** A carried item's worn instance is hidden by `renderer.enabled = false`, locally only; `BodySite.Dispose` runs unconditionally from `Exit`, `OnDisable` and death, whether or not there is still a lens to see through. Miss one and the item is invisible on that machine until it is re-worn — the pack made exactly that mistake once. `SetWornHidden` is phrased as "is this still the one we hid" because a destroyed instance answers null on both sides of a comparison.
- **The lens's wall probe reports a SURFACE distance, and its hit buffer grows.** `RaycastHit.distance` from a sphere cast is how far the sphere's *centre* travelled, already a radius short of the wall, and `LensStandoff.Resolve` subtracts a radius of its own for clearance — so `BodyFocusCamera.NearestBlocker` adds the radius back on. Hand it the raw value and the lens stops two radii out from a wall it was asked to come within one of, a framing error that never gets reported as a bug. And `SphereCastNonAlloc` drops arbitrary hits once its buffer is full, including sometimes the nearest: the screen is opened inside the ship as often as out on the sand, and the hull is a baked proxy of 420 convex hulls, so the buffer doubles and re-casts (to `MaxHits` 512) rather than answering from a partial list.
- **The gear screen stands the body down to a plain idle.** `BodyFocusSession` sets `PlayerAimRig.Relaxed` on the way in and clears it on the way out: the screen is a camera flown round to look AT the character, so a pose left over from whatever they were doing when they pressed I — arms out around a rifle, a gauntlet arm up, a torch held forward — puts the wrong subject in frame. It is deliberately not the same switch as the death rule beside it, which must never hand a pose back.
- **A site's ghost gives way to the preview, on all three.** `showPlaceholder` is `wantsAnchor` — empty, reserved, or refused-while-empty — so the moment a legal item is carried the stand-in is replaced by a translucent copy of that item, seated where it will land. That is the answer to "where does this go", and it is a better one than a lit fixture: on the torso it is the gear's own worn shape. The chest place borrows the same site and has no authored ghost at all, by design — the only way to reach it is to be holding a chest item, and the dim copy on the sternum is its signifier.
- **The empty torso shows `GhostBack`, and the lash rail is only where it SITS.** The rail used to be what lit up — outlined in place, with the ghost as its understudy for a deployed pack — and it was the weakest signifier of the three: a thin bar buried in the rig's rack, reading as "a bit of your luggage is highlighted" rather than as a slot. Now the frame is always the ghost, and `BodyEquipmentController.TorsoMount` *seats* it (through `WornSeat.Apply`, the same call that wears the real thing), which is what puts it over the shoulders with a pack on and at the fit's own offset without one — and, since 2026-09-04, that same rail is also **where the back place is clicked**, so the two answers come off one transform. If the ghost renders edge-on, flat or too low, fix `GearGhostBuilder.BackLocalEuler`/`BackLocalPosition` and rebuild — never the model. `VerifyFrame` measures the FBX (widest on X, tallest on Y, thinnest on Z) so the axis-conversion assumption cannot rot silently, and `VerifyGauntlet` refuses any mesh that is not a `Mesh_GhostDevice_*` part — which is what catches the ghost export being pointed back at `gauntlet_base.blend`, drawing a translucent bracer over the solid one the player is wearing.
- **The chips are placed by screen side, not by handedness** — the camera faces the player, so the player's LEFT arm is on the screen's RIGHT. `PlaceChips` compares each site's rect centre with the canvas centre; do not "fix" a chip on the wrong arm by swapping the slot.
- **A save restore reaches `Wear` AFTER `Start`, so a post-`Start` flag is not enough.** `BodyEquipmentSaveable.RestoreState` waits on `PlayerSaveSync`'s claim RPC, so it lands a frame later on the host and a full round trip on a client — after the adopt loop. Worse, on a *peer's* machine a restore is indistinguishable from any other replicated `NetworkList` delta: there is no local trace of the load at all. `BodyEquipmentController` therefore gates its wear feedback on `adopted` **and** a serialized settle window (`wearSettleSeconds`, 2 s), which is a heuristic and is documented as one — otherwise reloading a world clanks and throws both arms up, once per slot. An exact gate would have to put "this write was a restore" on the wire. Anything else that reacts to a slot change inherits this.
- **Worn gear is drawn on the gear screen only** — on the figure AND, since 2026-09-06, on that screen's own rail. What stays rejected is a strip of Q/E/back tiles beside the **HUD** hotbar: tried on 2026-09-02 and removed the same day, because on the plain HUD it read as loose inventory tiles lying about. The hotbar tile's corner disc still marks a worn kind stored there.
- **A gauntlet model must contain NO `Mesh_GauntletBase_*` object, and the bracer must contain the DECK.** Two halves of one rule, both silent. A device that still carries its own bracer copy draws a second one in the same place — two shells one inside the other, z-fighting across every face, which reads as a shader fault rather than a duplicated mesh; `strip_bracer.py` removed them from the seven built before 2026-09-04 and is re-runnable. Conversely, shipping the base's **Plain** variation instead of **Mount** loses `Deck` and `Bosses`, and *nothing looks broken*: devices are seated off the bone, not off the deck, so they still land correctly with simply nothing underneath them. `ForearmBracerBuilder.VerifyBracer` (Mount parts only) and the name check in `_gauntlet`/`strip_bracer` are what keep both from rotting. The two objects that legitimately leave the base namespace are renamed out of it so the check can be by name: the Sucker Puncher's rails (its own track, via `_gauntlet.append_rails`) and the item scanner's bracket (the deck, hand-rotated onto the flank *with* the console).
- **Nothing on the body may take "the first `LateUpdate`" as proof that `BodyEquipmentController.Start` has run.** It is true that Unity runs every `Start` of a frame before any `LateUpdate` of it — but only for a `Start` that runs at all, and this one returns immediately, disabling its own component, on a body with no `IBodyEquipment` (which is every body but `PlayerCharacterNetworked` — the base `PlayerCharacter` prefab has none, and there is a plain instance of it sitting in `world/persistentScene.unity`). `ForearmBracers` shipped on 2026-09-04 assuming the frame, took its one chance, missed, and **returned silently forever**: bare forearms on every player, no message, and a knock-on that looks nothing like it — an empty arm has no ghost either, so its site has no canvas rect, and a click aimed at a gauntlet falls through to whatever else the cursor is inside (the worn wing's box, which covers the arms). Poll for the site instead of assuming it, and **report when it never comes**; the error names the arm and which of the bone/socket pair is missing.
- **A gauntlet is seated on the FOREARM, never in the hand frame.** The hand frame aligns an item with the fingers and sizes it per item, so four cuff models once came out 7-10 cm off the forearm axis at four different scales — "does not fit as a gauntlet". There is no hand-socket fallback any more: a gauntlet prefab without a `GauntletFit` is refused with an error. `BodyEquipmentController.Strip` destroys a worn instance itself — it was never a socket's, and it is a different thing from `DisplayCopy.Strip`, which only makes a copy inert.
- **The dorsal direction is `thumbSide × armAxis` on a right arm, and the mirror on a left — in that order.** With the operands the other way round every gauntlet's deck sat on the PALM side of both arms, and a folded rest pose hides it completely: the model still hugs the arm, it is just upside down. Shipped that way for a day. Check it by putting a camera on the computed dorsal and looking for knuckles, or by asserting the proximal phalanges flex AWAY from it.
- **The models are authored at the suit's true size, so the scales are 1.** `components/props/gauntlet_base.blend` was measured off the skinned suit — every vertex bound at least half to the forearm or hand bone, binned 3 cm along the arm and 15° around it. The sleeve is 0.17-0.22 m from the bone and the bone runs near the PALM side, so the shell's section centre drifts 4.5 cm dorsal-ward from wrist to elbow. A gauntlet that needs a scale other than 1 is a gauntlet that was not authored against the base. The bracer itself is seated by the same component at the same 1, which is what makes "authored on the deck" and "lands on the deck" the same statement.
- **The raise is a clip, not IK — twice over.** An IK goal set in `OnAnimatorIK` is applied through the layer it is set on, and the Upper Body layer sits in `Empty` whenever the hands are empty (the ordinary case for a player wearing gauntlets), so two IK raises in a row (eye-frame hand goal, then a shoulder-anchored reach) showed nothing — "no changes", "it does not point at what I aim at". An IK raise had also been tried and rejected on look in Aug 2026. The `Point` clips give the layer something to play, and the pitch blend is what follows the crosshair. Symmetric arms: the `Raise Left` state is the right clips with `mirror` on. `PlayerAimRig` still lifts the layer weight to the raise blend so the state shows with empty hands.
- **Worn items must be unlimited or refilling** (`OnItemDepleted` on a worn item only warns; there is no server-side "remove from body" yet), and **hotbar key 4 is still bound** — `PlayerInventoryNetwork.SelectSlot` refuses indices past `inventorySize`, and without that a press cleared the hands for a slot that did not exist.
- **Starting items:** `startingItems` on the networked player is Hand items only (Jumping Rod); `startingBody` is `[WingPack, ItemScanner, null]`. A wrong-kind entry is skipped with a warning.
- **An FBX from `_exportlib` is ALREADY axis-converted; a −90° X on the parent is a second conversion.** Every mesh node in the imported model carries the Blender-to-Unity position (`(x, y, z) → (−x, z, −y)`) and its own −90° X, with the vertices left in Blender's axes. The nested-model line every item builder copies (`localRotation = Euler(-90, 0, 0)`) is therefore an EXTRA turn. On a hand-held model that is deliberate — it points the item's length out of the fist — but on anything authored in the wearer's frame it moves the model off the wearer, and it fails into something that still looks like gear: applied to the two worn models it put the wingsuit's wings at the waist pointing backwards and the ornithopter's 0.6 m below the shoulders. Both worn models are nested with **no rotation**. The flight wingsuit's `Model` still carries the −90° X and is left alone deliberately: its membranes are reparented onto arm bones with fits tuned by eye against it, and its case is the only part visible in the hand.
- **A name sweep over the prefab ROOT will hit the worn model too.** `WingsuitBuilder.SetWingRenderers` switches off anything named `*Membrane*` or `*Batten*`, and the worn model's parts are `Mesh_WingsuitWorn_Membrane_L` / `_Batten_L` — so swept from the root it left the worn wing as a yoke, two spars and two cuffs with nothing stretched between them. It is handed the flight model's transform now. Any name-based sweep in an item builder inherits this the moment that item grows a second model.
- **A form is a RE-SEAT with an ORDER, never a bare model swap — three ways to get it wrong, all silent.** (1) The two worn models have different spans and `WornSeat.Apply` is the only thing that turns a span into a scale, so switching the child alone leaves the gear screen's 5.51 m wings wearing the 2.64 m bundle's scale — the same wing at a third the size, and it looks deliberate. `BodyEquipmentController.SetTorsoForm` re-seats, and **remembers** the form, because the gear screen is *where gear is put on* and an item worn while it is open would otherwise be the one thing on it in the wrong shape. (2) It must **bracket** `BodySite`, not sit inside it: a site hides the worn item by switching its renderers off and holding the list, so a swap between that and `Dispose` strands the list on a child that is no longer showing and the item is **invisible for good on that machine** until re-worn — hence `Enter` swaps before building the sites and `Exit` after disposing them. (3) `Carried` must switch the gear-screen model off too: the old two-way swap turned on every renderer-bearing child that was not `WornModel`, which would have left `InspectModel` on in the player's fist *and measured with it* — the `ItemBounds` trap the wingsuit's flight wings shipped once. Both variant children are switched explicitly now, whether or not they draw.
- **`WornFit.anchorToBone` opts out of the pack rail, and back gear needs one answer or the other.** Rail-anchored is the default and is right for anything that clips to the rig — the wing pack's wings hang off the rail's two protruding bar tips, so centring the model on the rail is what puts them there. Bone-anchored is for gear shaped around the WEARER: the worn wingsuit's wing roots are the measured upper-arm joints, and the rail sits half a metre behind the spine, so seating it there would hang the wings off the back of the pack. It also decides whether deploying the pack moves the gear — rail-anchored gear falls back to the fit's own offset and shifts, bone-anchored gear does not, which is right for a suit.
- **Every worn model is authored at TRUE WEARER SCALE and its `WornFit` size is the authored span** — 2.64 m for the wing pack stowed, 5.51 m spread (`inspectSize`), 2.60 m for the wingsuit, all printed by the exporters. Left at 0 ("keep the prefab's own scale") a re-export that changed a size would silently move the gear off the body it was measured against; written down, it shows up as a value disagreeing with the exporter's printout. The models are measured off the game — rail tips at x = ±0.885 in the spine bone's frame, upper-arm joints at ±0.233, hip at −0.269 — so the seat has nothing left to guess, and two models sharing those origins is what makes swapping them free. Raising one to make gear *bigger* scales uniformly about the seat point and drags every root off the anatomy the model was measured onto — the wing pack's shoulder pivots off the rail's bar tips, the wingsuit's wing roots off the shoulders; both were tried on 2026-09-04 and both failed visibly and immediately.
- **`WingPackBuilder` writes the `WornFit` now.** It used to be YAML-edited into the prefab, which the builder then stripped on every re-run — the same way that builder once lost its `NetworkObject` and both savers. It went into the builder when the worn wings did, because the fit and the model it sizes have to be written by the same hand.
- **A back item worn while the pack was deployed keeps the authored offset until it is re-worn.** `WornSeat` reads the rail once, at wear time, and the item is parented to the spine — so gear put on with no pack on the back sits at the `WornFit`'s fallback position and stays a few centimetres off the rail when the pack comes home. Re-wearing it (any slot write) re-seats it. Live re-seating on every pack state change was not built: the two positions are close, the case is rare, and the machinery would have to run on every machine for every player.
- **The lash rail belongs to the backpack system too.** `SURF_LongGoods` is a pack surface *on that same rail*, so a 1.35 m laser staff and a wing pack are two systems claiming one bar. They do not collide today only because `IsExteriorWhenStowed` is `Rack` alone — everything lashed to the rail is folded inward and unreachable while the pack is worn. Make another surface exterior when stowed and this becomes a real overlap. See [Backpack.md](Backpack.md).
- **A torso swap that crosses between the two places draws the preview as well as the outline.** A swap normally says everything with an amber rim on what is already there; back and chest are on opposite sides of the body, so that rim is behind the player exactly when the lens is in front of them. `BodySite` detects the crossing (`carried.equipKind != wornKind`) and shows the preview at the new place too. Without it, dropping a chest device onto a worn wing pack gives no visible feedback at all.
- **The stance's own three fields hit the same trap, and were shipped through it.** `armsOut`, `armDroop` and `armForward` were added to `BodyFocusSession` with `armsOut = true` and a 45° droop in code — and `PlayerCharacter.prefab` had no keys for them, so the shipped screen would have run with the stance **off** and a droop of zero while every editor-time check of a freshly-instantiated prefab looked right. The keys are in the prefab's YAML now. This is not a `Shot`-only rule: **any** new serialized field on a component already saved in a prefab needs the YAML key as well as the C# default — `torsoHitMetres` (2026-09-04) got its key added in the same edit for exactly this reason.
- **Removing a `Shot` field is safe; adding one is not.** `OrbitSeconds` went out of the struct and out of `PlayerCharacter.prefab` together — Unity ignores a YAML key it no longer has a field for, so a stale one is only untidy. The other direction is the trap below.
- **A new field in `BodyFocusCamera.Shot` deserializes as ZERO on the player prefab, not as `Shot.Default`.** `Shot` is a serialized struct already saved in `PlayerCharacter.prefab`, and Unity fills a key missing from that YAML with the C# default — the `= Shot.Default` initializer only ever runs for a struct built fresh in code. `OrbitSeconds` was added this way and would have hard-cut the swing on the shipped prefab while reading as a deliberate design choice. Both halves are fixed: the key is in the prefab, and `BodyFocusCamera.OrbitSeconds` treats 0 as "this prefab predates the field". Any future `Shot` field inherits this — add the YAML key as well as the C# default.
- **Nothing schedules the swing — `FocusCamera` re-asks for the shot every frame.** `LensPosition()` and `LensYaw()` are read live in `LateUpdate` and never sampled at spawn, so moving the heading *is* moving the camera, and the wall probe follows because it sweeps along the same heading. `BodyFocusCamera.Update` exists only to ease the angle, and is safe to declare only because the base class has no `Update` of its own: Unity resolves a message to the most derived method of that name, so one here would silently replace a base one rather than add to it.
- Tests: `BodySlotRulesTests`, `GearMovesTests`, `UseSlotCodeTests`, `DoubleTapTests`, `WornPoseTests`, `GearSaveCodecTests`, and for the screen `BodySiteStateTests`, `GearRailLayoutTests`, `BodyOwnershipTests`, `LensStandoffTests`, `FocusFlightTests`, `DisplayCopyTests`, `WornSeatTests`, `ForearmSeatTests` — all in [Editor/Tests](Assets/Game/Editor/Tests), because they touch Assembly-CSharp types and an asmdef cannot reference it.

## Extending

**Make an item a gauntlet.** Set `equipKind` on its `Resources/Items` asset, then model **only the device**: a `models/gear/gauntlet_<device>.py` that stands its machinery on the hardpoint deck at `_gauntlet.BASE_DECK_Z` and appends **nothing** from `gauntlet_base.blend` — the player is already wearing the bracer, and a second copy inside the model would sit inside it. The contract is in `components/props/gauntlet_base.py`'s docstring and `gauntlet_base_BUILD.md`. Export to `Art/Models/Items/`, then either give it a builder or add a row to `GauntletReseat.Roster` naming the model and every node the prefab references (including any material the prefab overrides — see the item scanner's screen). Sizes come from `GauntletPrefab`: `holdSize` 0, `packSize` 0 (both mean "the size the artist built" — the 0.54 shrink existed for a bracer too bulky to lie on the pack mat), `GauntletFit` at 1. Do not author an `ItemGrip.rotationOffset` — that turns an item in the HAND frame, and a gauntlet is never in it. Register nothing: worn instances are never spawned.

**Make an item a back item.** Set `equipKind` to `Back` and add a `WornFit` to the prefab. For gear that clips to the rig, only the **rotation and size** need authoring: the position comes off the pack's lash rail whenever there is one, and the fit's `localPosition` is the fallback for a back with no pack on it. For gear shaped around the wearer instead, set `anchorToBone` and author the offset from the spine.

**Give it a different look worn.** Nest a second model as a child named exactly `WornModel`, **switched off on the asset**, and `WornVisual` does the rest — `WornSeat` turns it on and everything else with a renderer off. Author it at true wearer scale in the spine bone's frame, nest it with **no rotation** (the FBX is already converted), and pin `WornFit.size` to its measured span so a re-export cannot move it quietly. **A third model just for the gear screen** goes in beside it as `InspectModel` with its span in `inspectSize`, authored on the *same origins* so the swap moves nothing; omit both and that screen simply shows the worn model. Then look at it: *Tools ▸ SpaceGame ▸ Items ▸ Preview Worn Gear* renders every worn item onto a body with a shouldered rig in the gear screen's own stance. Nothing to register for the *worn* instance — those are never spawned — but a back item that can also be dropped is an ordinary world item and needs its network prefab entry like any other. Worked example: [Wingsuit.md](Wingsuit.md).

**Make an item a chest item.** Set `equipKind` to `Chest` and add a `WornFit`; the chest has no fixture, so all three of the fit's fields are the pose. It then shares the one torso slot with back gear, which is what makes them mutually exclusive. **No shipped item is `Chest` yet** — the mechanism landed ahead of its content, deliberately, because retagging an existing gauntlet would have taken it off a player's arm without being asked. The empty chest needs no ghost model: the only way to reach it is to be carrying a chest item, and the translucent copy of that item seated on the sternum is the signifier.

**Add a body slot.** Append to `BodySlot` (never renumber), widen `GearRef.BodySlotCount`, add its row to `BodySlotRules.Accepts`, give `BodyEquipmentController` a bone and a socket for it plus a read-only seam to hand them out, add its case to `BodySiteState.Resolve` if the rules need one, and build its `BodySite` in `BodyFocusSession.Enter` with an anchor and a placeholder prefab — plus a key chip in `BodyInventoryUI.EnsureChips`/`PlaceChips` and a row in `GearRailLayout.Build` (the rail's tile count is not derived from `BodySlotCount` — a fourth worn slot needs a place in the pyramid decided by hand, not appended to a row). Saves are positional by slot, so a new slot at the end reads old saves unchanged. `BodySite` deliberately has **no `AnchorPosition`**: nothing called it, and the equip sound plays from the worn instance's own transform inside `BodyEquipmentController`. If a later feature needs a per-site world point, add it then.
