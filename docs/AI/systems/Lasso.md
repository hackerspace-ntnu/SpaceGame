# Lasso

A throwable loop with its own Verlet rope. **Hold to twirl, release to throw** — the press starts the loop turning over the player's head and it opens as it winds; letting go throws it as far as it was wound. Catch an animal, fight it, and tie it off to a post — or **tap LMB next to a person to tie them up**: the thrower kneels and works the rope while the person is forced to their knees, over onto their face and hogtied, and then stands holding the lead. Walking drags them; LMB lets go; interact on them heaves them over your shoulder (see [Lifting.md](Lifting.md)).

**Scope:** [`Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Lasso/`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Lasso) (9 files), [LassoVerb.cs](Assets/Game/Scripts/Core/Multiplayer/Messaging/Vocabulary/LassoVerb.cs), [Lasso.prefab](Assets/Game/Prefabs/Items/Artifacts/Gadgets/Lasso.prefab), [LassoTests.cs](Assets/Game/Editor/Tests/LassoTests.cs).
**Related:** [Artifacts.md](Artifacts.md) · [LeashSystem.md](LeashSystem.md) (where a catch is handed off to) · [Multiplayer.md](Multiplayer.md) · [Persistence.md](Persistence.md) · [AgentSystem.md](AgentSystem.md)

**Not this system:** the **leash** is a rope tied between any two things and is a separate artifact. The **net gun**'s `Snare*` files are a parallel capture system that shares `LassoTether.EstimateMassOf` and nothing else.

## Model

- **The gesture is the item.** `IsContinuous => true`, `WantsHold => false`: the press starts a twirl, the **release** throws. A lasso that fires on the press is a rope gun.
- **Loft is flight time, never extra upward speed.** [`LassoThrow.SolveVelocity`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Lasso/LassoThrow.cs) picks an apex, derives the flight time from it, and solves the launch so the loop passes **through** the aim point on the way down. `throwSpeed` is a speed *cap*, not the pace-setter: when a throw would exceed it the flight is lengthened and the arc **re-solved**.
- **The loop is a lariat, and the rope ends on its knot.** [`LassoLoop`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Lasso/LassoLoop.cs) draws a shape pinched into a throat at the honda and bellied opposite it, and `Twirl`/`Fly`/`Ride` each **return that knot's world position** so [`LassoArtifact`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Lasso/LassoArtifact.cs) can end the cable on it. Drawn to the loop's centre instead — which is what it used to do — the rope dies in the hole with the loop hovering unattached around it. **Rope and loop are ONE line:** `LassoLoop` owns no renderer; it computes `Shape` (honda → round → honda, empty while hidden) and [`LassoRope`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Lasso/LassoRope.cs), bound with `Bind(lineRenderer, loop)`, draws its chain and then the loop on past the honda in the same `LineRenderer` — one width, one material, one braid running through the knot. The loop used to have its own renderer (`loopRenderer`, a nested `line (1)` prefab) and read as a ring hung on a rope.
- **The drawn radius is the catch radius.** [`LassoLoop.Radius`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Lasso/LassoLoop.cs) is what the arc is tested against and what the aim guide draws, so a fully wound loop genuinely has a wider mouth than a flicked one.
- **The catch is swept, not sampled** — a `SphereCast` between the head's last and current position, plus an overlap at the destination for the case of arriving already inside a collider.
- **The thrower sees the arc; everyone else sees the loop.** [`LassoAim`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Lasso/LassoAim.cs) draws an owner-only arc-and-ring guide from the same solver the throw uses. The twirling loop stays overhead for observers.
- **The catch cinches, then the animal fights.** [`LassoTether`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Lasso/LassoTether.cs) takes the creature's legs off its AI with `SuspendSelfDrive()` — **never** `agent.enabled = false`: the suspend *records* whether the agent was enabled, which matters because `Awake` parks an agent that wakes before a NavMesh exists under it — and drives them itself.
- **The loop closes ON the body.** At the catch every machine measures a [`LassoCollar`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Lasso/LassoCollar.cs) (`Attach`, not for a drag): one closed ring on the skin — **a person's waist with their arms pinned to their sides** (humanoid: centred between `Hips` and `Chest` at `waistAlongTrunk` 0.45, about the elbows; outline = skin weighted to the trunk bones; the arms, everything under `Left/RightUpperArm`, taken in where they lie within `armHug` (2×) of the waist's reach, so an arm held out or up is left out; rides `Spine`), a creature's neck (the bone named `*neck*` nearest the attach point — the nearest bone of any kind closed round an ostrich's shin and a Dunehorn's barrel; failing that, the nearest segment) — laid by the hogtie's own [`SkinCoil`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Leash/SkinCoil.cs) and riding that bone, so it follows the struggle without being measured again. `LassoLoop.Ride(…, collar, ropeStart)` draws the open loop in onto the ring over the cinch, then IS the ring; the honda is the ring point nearest the rope's start. A body with nothing to measure keeps the old `collarRadius` circle. Presentation on every machine, like the hogtie wrap: nothing about either is sent or saved, and the tether, tension and save still use `root + _attachOffset`. Measured (neck ring, 2026-10-13): Dunehorn 34 → 4.5 cm mean off the skin. The waist ring is NOT measured in play yet. A person's knot is at the waist too: `LassoStruggle.AttachFractionFor` answers `personAttachFraction` (0.6) for a humanoid, `attachFraction` (0.75, the neck) for anything else, so the rope goes taut against the height the ring is drawn at. **A drag has no loop at all**: the lead is tied straight onto the captive's wrap knot (`BoundBody.RopePoint`), so `Attach` hides the loop and `RideRoutine` ends the rope on the knot — shown, it had no collar to close onto and hung as the bare 0.45 m hoop round their wrists and ankles.
- **The heavier end wins.** `LassoArtifact.PlayerPullShare(targetMass, playerMass = 80)` is `public static` and a pure function of two masses, because the two ends of the rope run on two different machines and must agree without a message.
- **The rope is a contest with two opposing loops** ([`LassoTension`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Lasso/LassoTension.cs)). Straining pays line out and tires the animal; slack winds line back within reach and lets the animal recover. Held under strain long enough the rope **wears through and parts**. **A hoist takes no line:** while the rope climbs from the catch to the hand at `hoistSlope` (0.7, `AgentCarry.IsLift`) or steeper, the strain is the catch's weight, not the catch pulling, so `JudgeTension` publishes no `StrainOn` (it still wears). Overshoot does not say which end made it: before this a jetpack climbing off a catch read as the catch taking line, the rope paid out at `payOutSpeed` (3.2 m/s) as fast as the pilot rose and grew to its 26 m while the body stayed down — any new way for the HOLDER to load the rope needs the same exemption. **A caught creature can be lifted.** `LassoTether.Bind` makes the owned body dynamic **with gravity** (agents are authored kinematic, gravity off; both recorded and put back), so the rope's `Constrain` hoists it under a jetpack and it falls back when the rope goes slack. Let go while the rope still has it in the air (`hoisted` — pulled upward since it last touched ground — and no ground contact), `Release` leaves the body dynamic (`landing`) until a contact with a normal ≥ 0.5 up (or sleep) says it is down, then `GiveBack`: kinematic, gravity, legs, NavMesh `Warp`. Without that a body dropped more than 6 m up was handed back kinematic with no mesh in reach and hung in the sky. `touchingGround` is set by `OnCollisionEnter/Stay` (after each physics step) and read-and-cleared at the top of `FixedUpdate`; a sleeping body reports no contacts, so sleep counts as landed.
- **A catch ends somewhere.** Pressing Use while roped and aiming at a hitchable surface within `hitchRange` builds a real [`Leash`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Leash/Leash.cs) between the creature and that anchor and drops the lasso. Aiming at nothing still means "let go" — the same shape `LeashArtifact` gives the gesture. **The ground is not a hitch:** a `TerrainCollider`, or any surface facing up within `hitchGroundAngle` (45°) — a floor, a rock top — is refused, so the press lets go. A player looking down to drop a catch used to knot it to the sand with a leash.

- **A tap ties up whoever is next to you.** No rope out, press near any person — a player or an agent, dialogue or not — and `LassoArtifact.PersonToTie` (owner) picks them: the one under the crosshair if within `bindReach` (2.2 m **across the ground** to their nearest surface), else the nearest within `bindReach` and within `bindCone` (50°) of the look. The crosshair need not be on them — at that range a body fills the view below the eye, and a click that grazed past fell through to a throw. `B = BindVerb` (4), the person in `Target`. `TryAim` skips the thrower's own colliders. `Present` on every machine → `TieUp`: `StatusReceiver.EnsureOnBody` + `BoundBody.EnsureOn(…, tieSeconds, binder)` first (a body with no receiver drops the announcement), then the machine that `Decides` the body checks reach itself (closest collider point, +`RelaySlack` 1 m) and applies [`StatusKind.Bound`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Gameplay/Status/BoundStatus.cs) — helpless from the click. `Bound` is a `HeldStandingStatus` (a creature is suppressed by `AgentController`, a player held by `BodyHold.TakeStanding`) with **no clock: it ends when the body has lain STILL for `stillSecondsToEscape` (30 s)** — moved more than `stillTolerance` (0.3 m) restarts the count, so a captive kept moving (dragged, carried, swung on a jetpack) never works free. `BoundStatus.OnTick` measures it on every machine off the root; the deciding machine clears it. Also ended by a respawn (`ClearAll`) or a load.
- **The tie is MADE, over `tieSeconds` (2.6) + `standUpSeconds` (0.9).** `TieRoutine` on every machine draws the lasso's rope from the hand to `HogtieWrap.Tip` — the turn being laid. The thrower's machine plays `tieAction` (*Kneel Work*: kneel, working hands, stand) and holds them still (`PlayerMovement.StartHauling(0)`); at `tieSeconds` the action is stopped (its exit clip stands them up), and after `standUpSeconds` `FinishTie(takeLead)` gives their pace back and calls `ToggleLead` — **the thrower is left holding the lead**. Meanwhile [`BoundBody`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Lasso/BoundBody.cs) plays the takedown with [`TiedPose`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Lasso/TiedPose.cs) — four measured key poses: as found → kneeling (`kneelEnds` 0.3) → an accelerating fall onto the face (`fallEnds` 0.5) → wrists drawn to ankles (`drawnUpEnds` 0.9), head turned AWAY from the binder so the knot is at their hands — and winds [`HogtieWrap`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Leash/HogtieWrap.cs) on progressively with `Draw(reveal)` from `windStarts` (0.15). A press while tying is a `MissVerb`.
- **Lying hogtied is not lying still.** `TiedPose.Struggle`: breathing always; bouts of heaving (`boutSeconds` 1.6, rests of `restSeconds` 3.5, each started up to 40% of a rest late so bodies do not heave in step; periodic, so it never runs out) working knees, thighs and arms, rocking the body `rollDegrees` and lifting the head `headLift`; a body dragged faster than 0.3 m/s fights at least 0.7. **It fights harder the nearer it is to working free** (`BoundBody.EscapeProgress` from `BoundStatus`): bouts up to `escapeEffort` (2×) as hard and, near the end, never resting. `TiedCaptive` shows the same progress as its readout ("Working free"). As the ropes come off the pose blends back to the animator over `releaseSeconds` while `getUp` (*Get Up Mixamo*, starts face down) plays where this machine writes the animator.
- **A tied body is a real rigidbody, and the lead is a joint — Unity's physics, no scripts moving it.** While tied, `BoundBody` on the machine that OWNS the body (the server for an NPC, a player's own machine) takes the agent's legs (`SuspendSelfDrive`), makes the root's `Rigidbody` dynamic, heavy (`tiedBody.mass` 70 kg), damped and interpolated (a player keeps upright rotation so their camera does not tumble), with a low-friction lying capsule. `Attach` in drag mode calls `BoundBody.Hold(hand, anchor, dragRopeLength 2.8)`: a `ConfigurableJoint` from the knot to the dragger's rigidbody at the muzzle, `Limited` motion at the rope's length with a critically damped give (`ropeSpring` 4000 / `ropeDamper` 1000), `connectedMassScale` 0 so the hand is never pulled. So the body slides when walked away from, is lifted by a jetpack and is stopped by the ground. Everybody else sees the root through its NetworkTransform (all three rotation axes sync). On the thrower's machine the pace is capped at `WalkSpeed × dragSpeedShare` (0.6) — the weight; `ApplyOwnerPull` stays out of a joint drag. The rope is drawn to `BoundBody.RopePoint` — the wrap's knot. A leash hogtie (a ragdoll) is still towed by `LassoedBody` → `RagdollRig.Tow`. The reel is off while dragging. A drag neither wears nor pays out and ends on every machine when the tie does. **LMB while dragging lets go** (`ReleaseVerb`, everywhere).
- **Interact carries them over the shoulder.** `BoundBody` puts [`TiedCaptive`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Lasso/TiedCaptive.cs) on the body — an `IInteractable` ("Tied up" / "RMB: carry over your shoulder" / "RMB / Esc: put down" / "Too big to carry" / "Somebody is carrying them") offered only while `Bound`. Its `Interact` (presser's machine) always goes to [`CaptiveHaul`](Assets/Game/Scripts/Items/Artifacts/Lasso/CaptiveHaul.cs), the oxygen plant's carry with the captive draped over the right shoulder — see [Lifting.md](Lifting.md). A presser leading that captive on their own lasso (`LassoArtifact.Leads`) lets go of the lead first (`ToggleLead` → `LassoVerb.LetGo`); the only way onto the lead is the end of a tie. Its settings are the prefab's `haul` block. **The crosshair finds the body where it lies:** while tied `BoundBody` switches the body's own standing capsule(s) off (solid `CapsuleCollider`s on its root rigidbody) and lays a solid `TiedBody` capsule along the drawn body; both are given back exactly as found.
- **Every verb past the throw is prompted** (`PlayerHints`, id `lasso`, owner only, posted on change): "LMB: tie up", "Tying them up…", "Dragging · LMB: let go"; the pick-up prompt itself is the crosshair's, from `TiedCaptive`.
- **A rope on a catch can be cut by somebody else.** The laser staff parts it, and the lasso hears that as `LassoVerb.Snapped` — the same verb, the same sound, the same release, so the creature gets its legs back by the path it already had. A throw still in the air is deliberately not cuttable. See [RopeCutting.md](RopeCutting.md).

## Flows

**Wind up and throw**
1. Press → `OnRequestUse` sets `B = ThrowVerb` → `Present` on every machine → `BeginTwirl` (loop shown, `RopeTwirl` played).
2. Every frame, on every machine: `TickTwirl` accumulates `_twirlCharge` locally — every machine saw the press, and what the charge decides is already baked into the aimed point. The owner additionally draws `LassoAim`.
3. Release → owner's `OnRequestHold(active: false)` writes the aim point in `P` and the direction in `R` (`B` is unavailable — `EquipmentController` owns it on hold ticks as the active flag).
4. `PresentHold` on every machine → `ThrowRoutine` integrates the solved arc and pays the rope out behind it.
5. Only `OwnerIsLocal()` decides what was caught → `Attach` → `SendRope(Caught)`.

**The catch**
1. `LassoVerb.Caught` → server → `NetTo.All` → every machine runs `ApplyRope` → `Attach`, idempotent.
2. `Attach` resolves the knot height from the creature's own size, binds `LassoTether` (owner of the creature) or `LassoedBody` (the roped player's own machine), cinches the loop, cracks the rope and plays `RopeCatch`.

**The contest**
1. The **authority** measures the overshoot each `FixedUpdate`, wears the rope, and publishes `StrainOn`/`StrainOff` as edges.
2. Every machine integrates the rope length from those edges at the authored rate — reeling shortens, straining pays out — so all of them agree without a per-tick message.
3. Past the wear budget the authority sends `Snapped`; every machine plays `RopeSnap` and releases.

**Tie off**
1. Press while roped → `TryAimAtHitch` (owner) → `B = HitchVerb`, anchor in `Target`, knot in `P`, `BareAnchorFlag` when the anchor has no networked identity.
2. `Present` on every machine → `LassoHitch.TieOff` builds a `Leash` → `Release`. The leash is then saved and constrained by [LeashSystem](LeashSystem.md). Refused while dragging.

**Tie up**
1. Owner, no rope out: `PersonToTie` finds somebody within `bindReach` (crosshair, else the cone) → `B = BindVerb`, person in `Target`.
2. `Present` everywhere → `TieUp`: receiver + `BoundBody` ensured with `tieSeconds` and the binder, `RopeHitch` sound; the deciding machine re-checks reach and `Apply(Bound)`; `StatusSet` reaches every machine (and late joiners, re-announced by the receiver). `BoundBody.Show` starts the takedown wherever the condition lands.
3. `TieRoutine` everywhere: the rope from hand to `Wrap.Tip`. Owner: `tieAction` + pace 0, stop the action at `tieSeconds`, after `standUpSeconds` → `FinishTie(takeLead: true)` → `ToggleLead`.

**Drag**
1. `ToggleLead` (end of a tie) → `SendRope(Caught, captive)` → server → all → `Attach` in drag mode: `BoundBody.Hold` (the joint, on the body's owner), the owner's pace capped.
2. LMB (`ReleaseVerb`) or interact on the captive being dragged (`SendRope(LetGo)`, then the shoulder carry) → `Release` everywhere: `BoundBody.LetGo`, pace given back. `RideRoutine` also releases on every machine the moment the captive stops being tied.

## Multiplayer

- **`UseAuthority.Owner`**, and `Use()` is deliberately **empty**. Everything is built by `Present` on every machine; the constraint decides for itself which machine may run it.
- **Messages ride the THROWER's channel, never this item's.** The prefab carries a `NetworkObject` (it must — dropping routes through `World.Spawn`) but that object is never spawned while the item is in a hand, so a send from here resolves to a dormant relay and runs locally only. `NetMessaging.NetSendTo(owner, …)`.
- **`IsAuthority` (`!Network.IsNetworked || Network.Server`), not `Network.Simulates(this)`.** An equipped artifact is instantiated into a hand and never spawned, so `Simulates` answers "yes" on every machine at once.
- **`OwnsTarget()` asks ownership of the TARGET, not of the item.** A loose creature is owned by the server, a ridden mount by its **rider**.
- **Late joiners** get `Caught` re-announced from `OnPeerJoined` — it is absolute state, not an edge, so it costs a joiner one `Attach` and everyone else one no-op.
- **The tie sequence sends nothing new.** The takedown, the wind-on, the trailing and the lying collider are presentation on every machine from the replicated `Bound` condition and the replicated rope ends; the thrower's kneel and get-up travel on their `ClientNetworkAnimator`; the lead is the existing `Caught`. A machine where the condition arrives late starts its takedown late. **Verified 2026-10-13 in play mode offline only** (tap → tied → kneel/takedown → lead taken → captive dragged 5.4 m on the ground → LMB let go → untie restored the capsule); never on a client. **The hoist, the landing and the waist ring send nothing new** (2026-10-14). The hoist exemption only changes which `StrainOn`/`StrainOff` edges the authority publishes; gravity and `landing` run on the machine that owns the creature (where the tether lives) and reach everyone in its replicated transform; the ring is measured on every machine from `Caught` like the neck ring was. **Type-checked only — not run in play, on a client, or across a reload.**
- **Nothing spawned here is a network prefab.** The rope, the loop, the aim guide and the hitched leash are all local `Instantiate`/`new GameObject` on every machine.

## Persistence

- **The tie sequence adds no state worth saving**: a takedown, a lead and a lying collider all hang off the `Bound` condition, which is never saved, so a load stands the captive up with its own capsule. **A hoist adds nothing to save.** A body saved mid-hoist or mid-fall is a creature whose rope the save already records (or not); on load it is restored kinematic from its prefab and the agent's own recovery puts it on the mesh.
- **A drag is not saved.** `CaptureItemState` skips it: the tie it rides on is deliberately never persisted ([Hogtie](Hogtie.md)), so a restored drag would be a pelvis-height rope on a creature that has just been let up. Fatigue is not saved either; a restored catch is fresh.

- `IItemDeferredRestore`. `CaptureItemState` writes the target `SaveRef`, the attach offset and the rope length; an unreferenceable target writes **nothing** rather than a rope attached to nowhere.
- `TryCompleteRestore` is idempotent and stays pending until the creature's chunk streams in.
- Restore order is load-bearing: `Attach` first (it writes its own length from the current gap), then the saved offset and length, then `SendRope` — announcing earlier publishes the guess instead of the saved value.
- **A hitched creature needs nothing from this system.** Once tied off it is a `Leash`, and `LeashSaveable` captures it off `Leash.All`.

## Gotchas

- **A hand-typed GUID can collide with a real asset.** `LassoAim.material` once pointed at `7a1c4e9b…0b01`, a material that never existed, and the same GUID is `LeashEnd.cs`'s. Loading `Lasso.prefab` (any NPC whose loadout can draw the lasso) then logged `Failed to load …LeashEnd.cs. File may be corrupted`, which fails any test that loads it. The field is empty now: an empty material builds the runtime fallback.

- **Loft added to a solved arc is a miss, not a lob.** `throwArcHeight` used to be added straight onto the vertical component of a correct ballistic solution. That is a throw at a *different* target: at the prefab's shipped numbers the loop passed **1.6 m over** a creature aimed at from 12 m and **4.0 m over** one at 30 m, and crossed the target's own altitude a constant **13 m behind it** (`2·arc·speed/g`, which is why the error did not vary with range) — against a catch radius of 0.22–0.8 m. Nothing failed; the arc was real ballistics and the rope drew beautifully, and the item simply did not go where the crosshair was. `TheThrowLandsOnThePointItWasAimedAt` pins it, and `TheThrowArrivesOnTheWayDownAndActuallyArcs` pins the half that a flat rifle shot would otherwise satisfy.
- **A point-sampled catch tunnels.** A single `OverlapSphere` at the head's position samples a thing moving up to 22 m/s against a 0.22 m mouth. The sweep and the destination overlap are both needed: a `SphereCast` reports nothing for a collider it *started* inside, which is exactly the case of a loop arriving on top of an animal.
- **The first-person eye is at root + 1.45 m, and the twirl loop is at root + `twirlHeight`.** At the authored 2.1 m that put the loop 0.65 m *directly above the camera* — out of frame for the only person who needed to read the charge off it, with its lower arc skimming the near plane. `twirlForward` carries the sweep ahead of the body; the thrower reads the charge off `LassoAim` instead. **Do not "fix" a loop the thrower cannot see by lowering `twirlHeight`** — that puts it back on their ear, which is the defect the 2.1 m was chosen to fix.
- **The knot is one number, asked for in one place.** It used to be two: `LassoArtifact.npcAttachHeightOffset` drew the rope and `LassoStruggle.attachHeight` constrained it, both 1.2 m on the same prefab with nothing keeping them equal. It is now a **fraction of the creature's own height** (`LassoTether.AttachHeightFor`) — a flat metre value hangs the collar in mid-air above anything small and around the shin of a habitat-sized walker.
- **Judge strain on one machine and publish the edge.** Every machine measuring its own overshoot against its own interpolated copy of two moving ends gives every one of them a different rope length within seconds — permanently, because the length is what the constraint and the break verdict are both measured against.
- **`Sfx` is played at the moment, not at the button.** `UsableItem.PlayUse` plays `useSound` inside `Present`, which for this item is the **press** — so the item's one sound fired at the start of the wind-up and again on the press that dropped the rope, while the throw, the catch, the crack and the coil-back were silent. `useSound` on the prefab is now empty and the six `SfxId.Rope*` entries are played where they happen.
- **A LineRenderer's texture is Stretched by default.** The rope and loop were drawn in `Custom_Wood` — a surface material off a prop — fitted once across up to 26 m of cable. Both now take `Rope_Leash.mat` (the braid the leash already uses) with `LineTextureMode.Tile`, and neither casts shadows: a view-aligned ribbon casts the shadow of a flat strip that changes shape as the player turns their head.
- **A circle is not a rope loop, and a rope that stops in the middle of one is not tied to it.** The loop was 28 segments of `cos`/`sin` with a 10% wobble, centred on exactly the point the artifact drew the cable to — so it read as a hard hoop, and the two objects never touched. Both halves are one fix: the profile is pinched at `phi = 0` (`hondaPinch`, `belly`) and that vertex is handed back as the rope's far end. Two consequences worth knowing. The throat costs mouth: at the shipped 0.55/0.12 the largest circle that fits inside the drawn hole is **84% of `Radius`**, while the catch sphere is still the full `Radius` — it errs generous, which is the right direction, but the drawing no longer bounds the catch exactly. And `hondaPinch` is normalised by `ThroatPeak()` precisely so tightening the knot does not silently shrink the whole loop while `LassoAim` goes on drawing the old ring.
- **The honda rides the spin rather than facing the hand.** The obvious rule — put the knot at the rim point nearest the rope's origin — is degenerate exactly where the loop matters most: during the twirl the hand is almost straight below the loop's own plane, so the projection has no length and the knot flickers round the rim. Riding the phase is also what a turning loop actually does, and it is what puts the sweeping spoke between hand and loop.
- **`Mathf.Pow` of a negative base is NaN, and `sin(π)` is negative.** The throat term is `Pow(Sin(phi/2), hondaPinch)`, and at `phi = 2π` the sine lands a hair below zero in float. Unclamped that puts the loop's closing vertex — which is the knot, which is the rope's endpoint — at NaN, and a NaN in a LineRenderer takes the whole line with it.
- **`LassoRope.Simulate` pins its ends outside the substep loop as well as inside.** The substep is a fixed 90 Hz, so a frame drawn faster than that runs none at all and leaves the ends where the last one put them. That was invisible while the far end was a slow-moving head; it is a visible gap between rope and loop now that the far end is a knot on something turning at 620°/s.
- **`Show(start, start)` stacks every node on one point** with zero-length segments the solver cannot give a direction to. Seed along the aim.
- **Slack, not span, is the shape of a rope.** `FlightSlack` must stay well outside `Straighten`'s 0.9–1.0 band or the cable is snapped onto the chord every substep and the throw draws as a straight line. `ThrownRopeTrailsInACurve` and `RopeStaysSmoothWhileBeingThrown` pin both halves; either is trivial to satisfy alone by breaking the other.
- **A respawn drops the rope, at either end.** `LassoArtifact.Binds` answers for the holder as well as the catch, so a thrower who respawns lets go and a caught player who respawns is let go of — `RespawnRelease.Everything` → `CuttableRopes.CutEveryRopeOn` → the same `LassoVerb.Snapped` a rope tearing under strain sends. A throw still in the AIR binds nobody, exactly as it appends no span.
- **A release with no orientation is a cancel, not a throw.** `EndHold(send: false)` delivers a `default` NetArg on unequip, disable and death; `arg.HasOrientation` is the test.
- **A lasso captive LIES but is held STANDING; never route it through `Hogtie`.** `Hogtie` holds a body DOWN as a ragdoll and runs a struggle pool — the leash's tie. The lasso's is a status so it is neither and reaches late joiners; the lying is `TiedPose`, presentation only, so the root, collider, agent and the player's camera pivot all stay upright where they were.
- **Pose muscles are measured, not named.** On these rigs a positive "Front-Back" swings a limb BACK. The defaults were found by a scored search on Nomad and Raxy (hands and feet up behind the back and meeting); guessing the signs from the names bent the knees into the ground. Muscles go over a neutral pose, never the animator's, or the same numbers give a different hogtie per clip.
- **The ground is measured too.** A pose's body position is in the animator's scaled space, models sit at different heights above their roots (Nomad 0.56 m, Raxy 0.11 m) and face down the chest hangs below the hips: every estimate left one rig 0.7 m underground. `TiedPose` bakes the meshes standing and lying, once, and sets the lowest point on the feet' height.
- **`LassoedBody`'s share used to pass the masses the wrong way round** (`PlayerPullShare(throwerMass)`, the thrower's as the target's). It agreed only because every player weighs 80. With a dragged body of any weight it is `1 − PlayerPullShare(heldMass, holderMass)`, mirrored exactly by `ApplyOwnerPull`.
- **A drag must re-seat the root.** `RagdollRig.Tow` moves bones by position with outward speed removed, so they read as at rest; `IsSettled` then stops `FollowHips` and the root (save record, every watcher) stays where the body was tied. `Tow` resets the settle clock and calls `FollowHips` itself.
- **`AgentController.Motor` is null outside play mode** (resolved in `Awake`, which `AddComponent` does not raise in edit mode). `LassoTether.Bind` falls back to `GetComponentInParent<ISelfDrivingMotor>()` or every EditMode test silently fails to take hold.
- **Drive the animator from `LateUpdate`.** `AgentController.Update` feeds `AgentAnimatorDriver` `Motor.Velocity`, which with the agent suspended is zero — so a struggling creature slides with an idle animation.
- **Right mouse is shared — and it is also Interact** (`UI/RightClick` drives the reel and stays enabled during play alongside interact — see [InteractionSystem.md](InteractionSystem.md)). Reeling in, or leading, a character with dialogue used to talk to them on every press. `DialogInteraction` refuses anybody restrained (on a rope, held by a suppressing condition such as `Bound`, or down); any OTHER interactable on a person (a rideable mount's `MountModule`) still answers the same press as the lead.
- **`BoundBody` is only added where `TieUp` ran.** A late joiner gets the `Bound` condition — helpless — but stands there unroped, with no drag interaction. The same limit `ShockedVisual` has.
- **Scripted towing was replaced by the physics engine (2026-10-13).** A tow that wrote the root's position each fixed step (`LassoTether.BindTowed`, `LassoedBody`'s held-standing path, `BoundBody.Trail`/`FollowGround`) jittered against render frames, could not leave the ground — a jetpack pilot could not lift a captive — and needed a fix per terrain quirk. Do not bring it back: a joint on a dynamic, interpolated body does all of it.
- **The joint is built only where the body is owned.** Elsewhere the root is a kinematic replica that its NetworkTransform moves; a joint there would be a second authority. The hand's rigidbody on the owning machine is whatever copy of the dragger it has — on the server a client's kinematic replica, which drags a dynamic body perfectly well.
- **A rope's spring must be near critical damping.** At 2500/250 a Nomad yanked from rest slingshot 3 m past its dragger in play; 4000/1000 (≈ 2√(k·m) for 70 kg) settles without bouncing. 0 spring is a rigid rope.
- **Teleporting the dragger yanks the captive with it.** Anything that moves a player instantly (a respawn, `SaveTeleport`) should let go first; a respawn does (`RespawnRelease` cuts every rope).
- **Release stands the root up and puts the agent back on its mesh.** A body that rolled under physics is turned upright about its own heading, made kinematic again, given its legs back and `Warp`ed to the NavMesh within 6 m — in that order, because `Warp` only works on an enabled agent.
- **The lying body is offset from its root.** It lands `fallReach` × height ahead of where it stood (a fall pivots on the knees), turned by the yaw it went down with — so the root sits at the knot end. The `TiedBody` capsule is placed in the root's own space at `yaw × Reach`, and the joint is anchored at the knot, never the root.
- **Only the standing CAPSULES on the body's own rigidbody are switched off while it lies.** Not every collider: worn and carried gear has colliders on the same rigidbody, and re-enabling something another system had switched off meanwhile is a bug of its own.
- **A tap out of reach is a throw.** 2.2 m is to the nearest SURFACE — a Nomad 2.9 m away root to root (capsule radius 0.62) is 2.28 m out and gets the wind-up, not the tie. In play this read as "nothing happened" until the release.
- Prefab field names that must survive any rewrite or `Lasso.prefab` silently nulls them: `lineRenderer`, `muzzle`, `lassoModel`, `reelInAction`.
- **Never give the loop its own renderer again.** Two LineRenderers meeting at the honda are two view-aligned ribbons with two end caps, two texture starts and two independent turns towards the camera: the seam shows from every angle however carefully widths and materials are matched. `LassoRope.Redraw` also skips the loop's first point, which is the honda the chain is already pinned to — a zero-length segment has no direction for the ribbon to face and pinches the line at exactly the knot. `TheLoopIsDrawnOnTheRopesOwnLine` pins both.
- **Known, not fixed:** DuneRat/Sandloper loops ride over the mane spikes. And `RopeStaysSmoothWhileBeingThrown` fails (mean turn 65° in the first frames of a throw against an 8° limit) — the chain simulation, not the drawing; it predates the one-line change.

## Extending

- **A new rope state** is a `LassoVerb` (append only — retired ids still travel between builds) plus a case in `ApplyRope`. Make it absolute state rather than an edge if a late joiner has to learn it.
- **Retuning the tie:** `bindReach`, `bindCone`, `tieSeconds`, `tieAction`, `standUpSeconds`, `boundRope` (`HogtieWrapLook`: torso and limb turns, width, `looseLimit`, `limbHug`, fallback radii for a body with no mesh, span, knot), `collar` (`LassoCollar`: `band`, `looseLimit`, `limbHug`, `alongBone`, `waistAlongTrunk`, `armHug`), `hitchGroundAngle`, `tension.hoistSlope`, `struggle.personAttachFraction`, `tiedPose` (`TiedPoseLook`: hogtie, kneel and face-down muscles, the takedown's phase marks, the bouts, `escapeEffort`, `getUp`, `releaseSeconds`), `tiedBody` (`TiedBodyPhysics`: mass, friction, damping, rope spring/damper); the escape (`stillSecondsToEscape`, `stillTolerance`) is `BoundStatus`'s, on each `StatusReceiver`, `dragRange`, `dragWeightShare`, `dragRopeLength`, `dragSpeedShare` on the prefab's Hogtie foldout; fight scaling (`referenceMass`, `fightScaleRange`) on The Caught Creature. Keep `tieSeconds × drawnUpEnds` past the tie action's enter clip (1.7 s) or the body is tied before the thrower has knelt.
- **A new thing to do with a catch** should follow `LassoHitch`: decide owner-side in `OnRequestUse`, encode into `B` above `BareAnchorFlag`, act in `Present` on every machine, and end in `Release()` unconditionally so a machine that could not act still drops the rope.
- **Tuning** lives on the prefab in `LassoRope` / `LassoLoop` / `LassoStruggle` / `LassoTension` / `LassoAim` foldouts. Adding a knob means adding it to the serializable class, not to `LassoArtifact`.
- **Anything worth a test** goes in `Assets/Game/Editor/Tests/LassoTests.cs` (Editor/, not the asmdef'd EditMode folder — these touch `Assembly-CSharp`). Prefer the pure statics: `LassoThrow` and `LassoTension.Wear` are testable without a scene precisely so the two things that were silently wrong can be pinned.
