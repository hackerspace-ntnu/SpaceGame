# Saddle — build record

Riding and pack saddles for large creatures. Two variations in one file, because
they are variations of one thing:

| Collection | What it is | Status |
|---|---|---|
| `Coll_Saddle_Appa` | Riding saddle fitted to `models/creatures/appa.blend` | what was asked for |
| `Coll_Saddle_Pack` | Smaller cargo-only pad, no seat and no stirrups | built ahead |

Rebuild: `blender --background --python saddle.py -- --out models/gear/saddle.blend`
(the script refuses to overwrite an existing `.blend` — delete it first, and only
if it carries no hand edits).

## Fitted, not eyeballed

The panel's underside is a loft through Appa's **actual** back. `BACK` in
`saddle.py` is a raycast measurement — straight down onto every visible mesh of
`appa.blend`, on a 8 x 7 grid — so the panel follows his real cross-section
rather than a guessed curve.

Measuring **every visible mesh** rather than the body alone is the point: his
shoulder fur `Cube.015` spans x 0.25..2.05 and sits proud of the body, so a
panel fitted to `Cube.016` would have been buried in fur along its whole front
half.

**Where it sits.** Centred at his x = 1.62. Two earlier stations: 1.60 straddles
the shoulder hump, where the fur is still the top surface and the saddle reads as
perched rather than seated; 1.82 seats cleanly but sits far enough back to look
like luggage rather than a riding position. 1.62 is the front of the clean back,
just behind the hump. He still has fur there — he is a shaggy animal and a saddle
on one sits *in* the coat, which is correct.

Moving the station means moving `ORIGIN_Z` with it: the panel's underside is
lofted through `BACK` at `ORIGIN_X`, so a station change with the old height
leaves the panel floating or sunk. `SaddleSeat` in `AppaBuilder.cs` is the same
point in Unity and has to move too.

## Wrapping, not offsetting

`BACK` is a cast straight **down**, so it knows only his top surface. The panel
and seat were right from the start; everything hanging off his sides was not.

Two offset schemes were tried and both are visible in a render:

1. A fixed drop below the panel's edge — every strap inside him, the girth by
   0.9 m, because he swells from 0.66 m half-width at the spine to 1.12 m half a
   metre lower.
2. Pushed outboard of his widest point — nothing buried, but the boards and
   skirts stood off him like shelves. A saddle is not a shelf; leather lies ON
   an animal.

What works is neither: build each piece flat at a nominal width and **wrap** it.
`wrap()` moves each vertex sideways only, onto the measured surface plus a small
gap, so the piece keeps its own shape and gains his curve.

`appa_surface.py` is the surface — a 35 x 27 grid of his half-width, generated by
`measure_appa_flank.py` and never hand-edited. Regenerate it if his mesh changes.

**A fitting must come off the wrapped geometry.** Once leather has been
projected it is no longer where the arithmetic that placed it said it would be,
so the stirrup irons and D-rings — positioned by a second, independent lookup —
hung on his flank with nothing joining them to their straps. `hanging_end()`
reports where a strap actually ended up, and the ironwork hangs off that.

**He is shaggy, and the measured surface is his coat**, not his skin. Straps
wrapped with a 8 mm gap disappeared into the fur; they sit at 30 mm.

## Fitting the sides, not just the back

`BACK` is a cast straight **down**, so it knows only his top surface. The panel
and seat were right from the start; everything that hangs off the sides was not.
Skirts, girth, D-rings, panniers and stirrups were all placed a fixed distance
below the panel's edge, and he swells from 0.66 m half-width at the spine to
1.12 m half a metre lower — so every one of them was inside him, the girth by
0.9 m.

`SIDE` is the matching **horizontal** measurement: half-width on a 9 x 8 grid of
(fore-aft, depth). `flank_out(sy, z_top, z_bottom)` returns where a part must sit
to clear him, taking the **widest** point over the span it covers — on a barrel,
a strap flush at its top edge is buried by its bottom one.

**`_sample` needs an ASCENDING axis.** `SIDE_Z` was first written the way it was
measured, deepest last, and every lookup then took the `q <= axis[0]` early
return and answered with the width at the spine. Nothing errored; the straps
simply went back inside him and the rebuild looked plausible. The table is stored
deepest-first for this reason.

Verified by casting from every saddle vertex in 26 directions and counting the
ones that cannot reach open air: 382 buried before, 112 after, and what remains
is the panel underside and the inner faces that are *supposed* to lie against
him.

## Frames

The saddle is authored in the **library** frame (−Y forward, +Z up); Appa's file
is −X forward. Placing one on the other is a rotation about Z:

    appa = ( 1.62 + saddle.y,  -saddle.x,  0.329 + saddle.z )

`ORIGIN_X` / `ORIGIN_Z` put the saddle's origin on his spine at the seat's
centre, which is the point Unity parents to the bone. In the saddle's own frame
**+X is his right** (up × forward), so the `_L` / `_R` object suffixes are named
from the animal's point of view.

## Decomposition

One file, no shared components — every part is specific to this saddle's fitted
curve, and a "saddle panel" that fits nothing in particular is not reusable.
Parts, all in `Coll_Saddle_Appa`:

| Object | Role |
|---|---|
| `Mesh_SaddlePanel_Appa` | The conforming underside. The only part that touches him. |
| `Mesh_SaddleSeat_Appa` | Dished seat lofted on top of the panel. |
| `Mesh_SaddleEnds_Appa` | Pommel and cantle — the humps that frame the rider. |
| `Mesh_SaddleSkirt_Appa_L/R` | Leather skirts down the flanks. |
| `Mesh_SaddlePannier_Appa_L/R` | The flat boards gear is laid on. |
| `Mesh_SaddleGear_Appa_L/R` | Girth webbing and the brass D-rings. |
| `Mesh_SaddleStirrup_Appa_L/R` | Stirrup leathers and irons. |

**The pannier boards are deliberately horizontal**, not flush to the flank.
`PackSurface` maps a uv onto a plane, so a board that followed the body's curve
would put every stowed item on a slope.

Empties Unity reads: `SEAT_Rider` (where `MountModule.seatOffset` aims),
`SURF_SaddleLeft`, `SURF_SaddleRight`, `SURF_SaddleRear` — the three pack faces.
`PackContainer` resolves to every `PackSurface` in its children when its list is
left empty, so those three empties are the whole wiring.

`SEAT_LIFT` (0.09) raises the seat block, the pommel, the cantle and
`SEAT_Rider` together, so the dish keeps its shape while the rider sits higher
on him rather than level with his spine. `AppaBuilder.SeatRise` is the same
number read back off the export and must move with it.

6,444 tris.

## Materials — and a blocker

**`palette.blend` cannot be read by Blender 4.2**, which is the only Blender
installed on this machine. Both `bpy.ops.wm.open_mainfile` and
`bpy.data.libraries.load` refuse it with *"not a blend file"*, though the file is
committed (`e04cca0b`) and is valid Zstandard data. It was presumably written by
a newer Blender.

So `_buildlib.link_materials` could not be used. `saddle.py` creates the five
materials locally instead, under **the palette's own names** and with the exact
values `PALETTE.md` documents, so replacing the block with one
`link_materials(MATERIALS)` call is all that is needed once the palette opens
again.

| Material | Used for | From |
|---|---|---|
| `Mat_Hide_Leather_Saddle` | panel, seat, pommel, cantle, skirts | **new** |
| `Mat_Fabric_Canvas_Faded` | girth and stirrup webbing | palette |
| `Mat_Metal_Brass_Tarnished` | D-rings, stirrup irons | palette |
| `Mat_Fabric_Canvas_Sand` | pannier boards | palette |
| `Mat_Wood_Ply_Worn` | board edging | palette |

`Mat_Hide_Leather_Saddle` (#6B4A2E, roughness 0.60) is genuinely new: the Hide
family has keratin (`Claw_Horn`, `Plate_Tan`, `Ivory_Spine`) and creature skin
(`Sand_Pale`, `Dune_Tan`, `Slate_Teal`) but no tanned leather, and the nearest
candidates are wrong by *kind* rather than by colour — `Mat_Fabric_Seat_Ochre` is
cracked vinyl upholstery, `Mat_Wood_Ply_Worn` is scavenged plywood. It could not
be added through `scripts/palette.py` for the same reason, so **it is not in
`PALETTE.md` yet** and must be added when the palette is fixed.

## Not done

No armature: nothing on the saddle articulates. Stirrups would swing on a real
one; if they ever need to, that is a two-bone addition, not a rebuild.

Nothing pre-existing was modified. `appa.blend` was opened read-only to measure
and to render the fit, and never saved.
